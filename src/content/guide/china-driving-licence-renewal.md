A Chinese driving licence carries its own expiry date, and renewing it is a separate procedure from converting a foreign licence. The medical check is part of the renewal, not an optional extra. Start before the expiry date printed on the card, because a lapsed licence is handled differently.

## Renewal is not conversion

Conversion is the one-time process of exchanging a foreign licence for a Chinese one. Renewal is what you do afterwards, each time the Chinese licence reaches its expiry date. Once your Chinese licence has been issued, your foreign licence is not a substitute for it, even if it is still valid in your home country. If you are stopped or need to show a licence, the Chinese one is the document that counts.
Treat renewal as its own task with its own steps. Do not assume the paperwork from your original conversion will carry over.

## The renewal window and the medical check

The renewal window opens before the expiry date, not on it. Check the expiry date on your licence as soon as you settle into your new posting, and plan the renewal inside that window rather than after it.
The medical check is a required part of the renewal. It is not something you can skip or do later as a separate errand. Arrange it as part of the same process, and keep the result with your renewal paperwork.

## If the licence has already lapsed

A licence that has passed its expiry date is not renewed in the same way as one still inside its window. The office treats a lapsed licence as a different case, so do not walk in expecting the standard renewal to apply.
If your licence has lapsed, ask the office handling your renewal what applies to your situation before you pay for a medical check or take time off work. The answer depends on how long it has been lapsed and on local practice, and it is not something this guide can settle for you.

## What to do at the office

Bring your passport, your residence permit, your current Chinese licence and the medical check result. Confirm the address and opening hours of the office that handles renewals where you live before you go, since this is handled locally.
This is a description of the procedure, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Requirements and handling vary by city, so confirm the details with the local office before you rely on them.
