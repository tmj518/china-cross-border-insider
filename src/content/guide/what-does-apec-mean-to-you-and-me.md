The APEC Business Travel Card is a document that simplifies entry into participating Asia-Pacific economies for business travelers. For someone relocating, it is not a visa or a residence permit, but it can affect how you move across borders in the region. This guide explains what the card does and does not do, so you can decide whether to pursue one.

## What the APEC Card Is

The APEC Business Travel Card is a credit-card-sized document that allows pre-cleared business travelers to enter and exit participating economies without applying for a separate visa for each trip. It is valid for multiple entries over a set period, which is typically several years.
The card is issued by your home economy's immigration authority, not by APEC itself. You must be a citizen or permanent resident of an APEC member economy to apply.

## What the Card Does Not Do

The card does not grant the right to work, settle, or reside in any economy. It is strictly for short-term business visits, such as attending meetings or negotiating contracts.
It does not replace a work permit or a residence visa. If your relocation involves taking up employment or long-term stay, you will still need the appropriate immigration documents for your destination economy.

## How the Card Affects Your Relocation

If you are relocating for work, the card may be useful for frequent business travel within the APEC region before or after you settle. However, it does not help with the relocation itself—your employer or you will still need to handle the standard visa and permit processes for your new country of residence.
The card can save time at border crossings in participating economies, as you may use dedicated lanes at airports. But this convenience only applies to the economies that have fully implemented the card scheme.

## Deciding Whether to Apply

Consider the card if your job will involve regular short business trips to multiple APEC economies. If your travel is mostly to one country or is for personal reasons, the card offers little benefit.
Check whether your home economy participates in the scheme and whether your destination economy is a full member. Some economies have restrictions on which nationalities can apply.
