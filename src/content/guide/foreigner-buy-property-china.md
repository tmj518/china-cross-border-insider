This guide walks you through the process of buying property in China as a foreigner, from verifying your eligibility to closing the deal and claiming any subsidies. It is not legal, tax, or immigration advice. Follow the steps in order.

## Before You Start

Confirm your residency status and work permit, as these determine your purchase rights. Rules vary by city, so check the local housing authority's requirements.
Understand that subsidies, if any, are city-specific and often tied to your employment or residency duration. You must apply for them separately after purchase.

## Eligibility Check

You must have lived and worked in China for a continuous period, and your work permit must be valid. Some cities require proof of social insurance payments.
You cannot buy more than one residential property for personal use, and commercial property may have different rules. Verify with the local housing bureau.

## Purchase Process

Find a property and sign a preliminary agreement. You will need your passport, residence permit, and work permit for the contract.
The seller or developer will handle the transfer registration, but you must be present to sign. Payment is typically made in installments or as a lump sum, depending on the contract.

## Subsidies and Aftercare

After the transfer, check if your city offers subsidies for foreign buyers, such as tax rebates or down payment assistance. These are not automatic—you must file a claim.
Keep all documents, including the purchase contract and payment receipts, for tax and subsidy applications. Deadlines for claims are strict, so act promptly.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
