The plan covers eldercare and childcare services, and the details that matter to you are local. No figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so this guide gives you the order of checks rather than numbers. Treat every cost and timeline you are quoted as something to confirm in writing.

## What the plan changes for a relocating household

The plan is about improving eldercare and childcare services. For you, that means the services your household would use after arrival, not a single national entitlement you can price from abroad.
What is actually available depends on your city, your district and your registration status. Ask about the specific service you need rather than the plan in general.

## Check your eligibility before you commit

Eligibility for subsidised or public eldercare and childcare usually turns on local residency registration and on the status of the person receiving care. Confirm which category you fall into with the district office where you intend to live.
If a family member needs care, ask whether their status travels with them or has to be established after arrival. Get the answer from the office that would process the application, not from a relocation agent.

## Price one application, not the plan

Costs sit with the individual application: enrolment, assessment, or a place at a facility. Ask for the fee schedule for the exact service you would use and for what is excluded.
Because no figure here could be verified, do not budget from this guide. Collect written quotes and compare them against what the district office tells you.

## What this guide is not

This is not legal, tax or immigration advice. It does not tell you whether you qualify for anything.
Use it to organise your questions, then put those questions to the responsible office and to a qualified adviser.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-26; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
