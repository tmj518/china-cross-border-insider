A reader in Lagos, Karachi or Mumbai typing "how much is a China business visa fee" is asking a reasonable question, and the honest answer is that no single national figure answers it. The fee is set by the mission or visa application service centre that receives the application, and it differs by the country you apply from and by the number of entries you request. This article does not give you a number, because the official pages that would carry one were not reachable when this was written. It gives you the procedure instead: who applies, what you bring, the order of the steps, and the reasons an application comes back.

## Definition

The China business visa is the M-category visa issued to a foreign national travelling to China for commercial and trade activities, and the fee is the charge levied by the Chinese embassy, consulate or authorised visa application service centre in the applicant's country of residence at the time the application is lodged.

## What the M visa is for, and who it applies to

The M visa covers commercial and trade activity: meeting a supplier, inspecting goods before shipment, attending a trade fair, negotiating a contract, or conducting technical and business discussions. It is not a work permit and it does not authorise paid employment in China. A person who intends to take up a post inside China is on a different track entirely, and the business visa is not a substitute for it.

The practical consequence for an importer is that the visa is tied to a commercial purpose you can document. The purpose is what the officer assesses, and the fee is downstream of that assessment, not the other way round.

## The documents that carry the application

The file you assemble is the part of the process you control. In the ordinary case it contains:

- A passport valid for the period the mission requires, with blank pages for the visa.
- A completed visa application form, signed, with a recent photograph to the mission's specification.
- An invitation from the Chinese trading partner or host company, on its letterhead, stating the commercial purpose, the dates, and who is receiving you.
- Evidence of the commercial relationship, such as a contract, a purchase order, or correspondence about the transaction.
- Proof of travel arrangements and accommodation for the period of the visit.
- Evidence of the applicant's own standing, such as an employer letter or business registration, depending on whether the traveller is an employee or a proprietor.

The invitation letter is the document most often treated as a formality and most often the reason an application stalls. It has to describe a commercial purpose that matches the visa category, and it has to come from a real counterparty.

## Steps

The sequence matters because each step produces the input for the next.

1. Confirm which mission or visa application service centre has jurisdiction over your place of residence. Applications are lodged where you live, not where you would prefer to travel from.
2. Establish the fee and the service charge that the centre handling your application publishes, and the method of payment it accepts. This is the step where the number you are looking for actually lives, and it is specific to that office.
3. Obtain the invitation letter from the Chinese host before finalising the form, because the form asks for the host's details and the dates of the visit.
4. Complete and sign the application form, and attach the photograph to the specification the mission publishes.
5. Assemble the supporting file: passport, invitation, commercial evidence, travel and accommodation proof, and evidence of your own standing.
6. Submit the application and biometrics if the centre requires them, and retain the receipt.
7. Track the application against the reference the centre issues, and collect the passport when it is released.

## What blocks an application, and why files are sent back

An application is returned or refused for reasons that are mostly about the file rather than about the traveller. The recurring ones:

- The stated purpose of travel does not match the visa category applied for, most commonly a traveller describing work when the file supports only commercial meetings.
- The invitation letter is missing, undated, unsigned, or does not identify the host and the commercial purpose.
- The passport does not meet the validity or blank-page condition the mission sets.
- The form is incomplete or inconsistent with the supporting documents, for instance dates that do not match the invitation.
- The applicant has lodged at a mission that does not have jurisdiction over their residence.
- The photograph does not meet the specification, which is a small error that stops the file at intake.

None of these are about the fee. They are about whether the officer can see a documented commercial purpose, and fixing them is cheaper than reapplying.

## Why this article has no figure

The fee is published by the individual mission or visa application service centre, and the pages that would carry the figure for a given country were not retrievable when this was written. Rather than quote a number from memory or from a secondary summary, this article states the procedure and leaves the arithmetic to the office that will actually take your payment. A number without a fetched source is worse than no number, because a traveller budgets against it.

## How confident

- **What we know:** The M visa is the business and commercial category, and the fee is levied by the mission or visa application service centre handling the application rather than set as one national price. The document set and the step order above follow the standard application procedure, and the blocking conditions are the ordinary intake failures.
- **What we do not know:** The amount of the fee for any particular country, the number of entries and validity options offered, the service charge added by visa application service centres, and whether any bilateral arrangement changes the charge for a given nationality. The official pages that would carry these were not reachable at the time of writing, so no figure is stated.
- **How to check:** Go to the website of the Chinese embassy or consulate with jurisdiction over your residence, or the visa application service centre it authorises, and read the fee schedule published there for your nationality and the number of entries you need. That page, not this one, is the authority for the amount.
