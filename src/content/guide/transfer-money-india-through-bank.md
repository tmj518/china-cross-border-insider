A bank transfer from China to India is handled at the counter of your Chinese bank, not online, and it is judged on paperwork before it is judged on anything else. The steps below are the order a branch normally works in. Nothing here is legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure in this guide is quoted because none could be verified against a source.

## Before you go to the branch

Confirm with your own bank that it handles outward remittances to India and which of its branches does. Not every branch processes foreign currency sends, and being sent to another branch wastes a trip.
Ask what the receiving bank in India needs on its side: the exact account name, account number, the bank's branch details and its SWIFT code. A single mismatch in the beneficiary name is a common reason a transfer is returned.
Check that your passport and your Chinese residence permit are both valid and that the names on them match the name on your bank account. If you have changed address or employer since the account was opened, update the bank record first.

## Documents to carry

Bring your passport, your residence permit and the bank card or passbook for the account you are sending from. Bring the beneficiary details written out, not stored only on your phone.
Bring the document that explains why the money is moving. For a family transfer that is normally a relationship document; for a payment for goods or services it is the contract or invoice. The bank decides what it will accept, so ask before the visit rather than argue at the counter.
If the money is yours and already in the account, say so plainly and be ready to show where it came from. Banks ask about the source of funds, and a vague answer stalls the application.

## At the counter

Tell the clerk you want an outward remittance to India and hand over the documents together. The clerk will complete the application form with you and will ask you to sign it.
Read the form before signing. Check the beneficiary name, the account number, the SWIFT code and the currency. Once signed, corrections usually mean starting again.
Ask for the receipt and keep it. It carries the reference you will need for every later question.

## After you leave the branch

The transfer is not finished when you walk out. It passes through checks at your bank and then through the receiving bank, and either can come back with a question.
Watch for calls from the branch and answer them. An unanswered query is treated as an unfinished application, and the money sits where it is until you respond.
Tell the recipient in India to expect the funds and to check with their own bank if nothing appears. Ask them to confirm the amount that actually arrives, because charges taken along the route are deducted before the money lands.

## If the transfer is refused or returned

Ask the branch which document or which detail caused the problem, and write the answer down. The same application can usually be resubmitted once that point is fixed.
If the money is returned, confirm with the branch that it has reached your account again and check the amount, since charges may still have been taken.
Keep every receipt and form from the failed attempt. They are what you show when you resubmit.
