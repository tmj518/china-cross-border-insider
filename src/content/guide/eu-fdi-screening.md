Several EU countries screen foreign direct investment before a deal completes, and the screening can hold up your relocation if you close first. This is the order to work through it. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice, and the fact sheet for this topic is empty, so no figure here is verified.

## What screening covers

Screening is a national review of a foreign investment before it is allowed to complete. It applies to acquisitions and to some other transactions that give a foreign investor control or influence over a target in the country.
Each country sets its own scope, its own thresholds and its own process. The European framework coordinates the national reviews; it does not replace them. Your filing is made to the country where the target sits, not to Brussels.
The sectors that attract review are typically those tied to security or public order. The list differs by country, so read the national rules for the country you are moving into.

## Check before you sign

Identify the country whose rules apply. That is normally where the target company or asset is located, not where you are moving from.
Read that country's screening rules and decide whether your transaction falls inside them. Look at the activity of the target, the size of the stake you are taking and whether control or influence passes to you.
If the transaction may be caught, plan the filing into your timetable before you commit to a completion date. A completion date set before screening is resolved is the most common way this goes wrong.

## File and respond

Submit the notification to the national authority in the form it requires. Expect to describe the investor, the target, the ownership chain and the purpose of the investment.
The authority may ask follow-up questions. Answer them in the form and language requested, and keep your answers consistent with what you filed.
Screening can end in clearance, in clearance with conditions, or in a prohibition. Conditions can bind how you run the business after completion, so read them before you accept them.

## Close only after clearance

Do not complete the transaction while screening is open unless the national rules expressly allow it. Completing early can expose the deal to unwinding and to penalties.
Build the screening outcome into your relocation plan. Your move, your residence steps and your local hiring should follow the clearance, not run ahead of it.
Keep the clearance decision and any conditions with your transaction records. You will need them when you set up the local entity and when you deal with banks.

## Confirm before you act
