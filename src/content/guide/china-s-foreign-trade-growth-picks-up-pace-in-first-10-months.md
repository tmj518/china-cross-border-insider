China's foreign trade has been expanding faster in recent months. If you are relocating, this affects shipping times, customs clearance, and the availability of goods. Here is what to do before you move.

## Why Trade Growth Matters for Your Relocation

Higher trade volumes mean busier ports and more cargo flights, which can delay your household goods shipment.
Customs processing may take longer as authorities handle more declarations. Plan for potential delays.
Demand for shipping containers and freight services rises, so book early to secure space and better rates.

## Check Current Shipping Conditions

Contact your freight forwarder or moving company to ask about current port congestion and transit times.
Ask if they have seen recent delays on routes from your origin to your destination city in China.
If you ship personal effects, confirm whether your items are subject to inspection or quarantine due to increased trade activity.

## Adjust Your Moving Timeline

Assume your shipment may take longer than the standard estimate. Build in extra buffer time before you need your belongings.
If you are moving for work, coordinate with your employer to ensure you have essentials (like work clothes and documents) in your carry-on luggage.
Consider shipping urgent items by air freight if sea freight is delayed, but weigh the higher cost.

## Prepare Customs Documentation Carefully

With more trade, customs officials are stricter about paperwork. Incomplete forms cause holds.
Prepare a detailed inventory of every item, with values and descriptions. Include receipts for high-value goods.
Ensure your passport, visa, and residence permit are valid for the entire customs clearance period.

## Monitor Trade News Before You Move

Trade growth can shift quickly. Check official sources for monthly trade data to anticipate further changes.
If trade accelerates, expect more congestion. If it slows, delays may ease.
Stay flexible: have a backup plan for temporary housing and essential purchases if your shipment is late.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-10; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
