The APEC Business Travel Card (ABTC) is for business travelers who frequently move between APEC economies. It gives you pre-cleared, faster immigration processing at participating airports and lets you stay longer without a visa. This guide walks you through the application process for one card.

## Check Your Eligibility

The card is for business people, not tourists or employees of non-business entities. You must be a citizen of an APEC member economy and have a genuine business need to travel regularly within the region.
Your employer must support the application, and you must have a clean criminal record. If you are a government official or a businessperson with a strong trade record, you are more likely to qualify.

## Gather Required Documents

You will need a valid passport, a completed application form, a recent photograph, and a letter from your employer explaining your business travel needs. Some economies also ask for proof of business registration or trade history.
Prepare copies of your passport bio page and any existing visas. Keep originals ready for verification at the application center.

## Submit Your Application

Applications are submitted through your home economy's immigration or trade authority. You cannot apply directly to APEC; each member economy processes its own citizens' applications.
Pay the application fee, which varies by economy, and submit your documents in person or online as required. After submission, your application is reviewed by your home economy, then sent to all other member economies for approval.

## Wait for Approval and Receive Card

The approval process can take several months because every member economy must vet your application. You will be notified when your card is ready for collection or delivery.
Once you receive the card, check that your name, passport number, and photo are correct. The card is valid for a set period, after which you must renew it.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-21; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
