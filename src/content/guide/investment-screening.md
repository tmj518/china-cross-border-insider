If you are moving to China and putting money into a company, a fund or a property project, your money may be reviewed before it can be used. The review is run by Chinese authorities, not by you, and the rules differ by sector and by where the money comes from. This guide sets out the order of actions. It does not tell you whether your deal will pass, and it is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What screening covers

Screening is a review of a proposed investment before it is completed. It looks at who is investing, where the money comes from, what the target company does, and whether the investment touches sectors the state treats as sensitive.
The scope is set by Chinese rules that change, and by the sector your target operates in. Do not assume a deal is outside screening because it is small or because the target is private. Ask a Chinese lawyer to confirm the current position for your specific sector before you commit money.

## Before you commit money

The most expensive mistake is signing and paying before anyone has checked whether the deal needs clearance. Once funds have moved, unwinding a blocked deal costs time and money.
Build the screening question into your due diligence, not after it. Your lawyer and your counterparty's lawyer should both confirm in writing which regulator, if any, would review the deal.

## When no figure can be confirmed

Timelines and thresholds for screening are not published in a form you can rely on, and they differ by sector and by the route your money takes. Treat any specific number you hear from a broker or a counterparty as unverified.
Plan around the process rather than a date. Keep your funds available, keep your documents current, and do not book travel, staff or premises on the assumption that clearance arrives by a particular point.

## If the investment is blocked or delayed

A block is not always final. You may be able to restructure the deal, change the investor entity, reduce the stake, or provide more information about the source of funds.
Get the reasons in writing through your lawyer before you change anything. Restructuring without understanding the objection usually produces a second refusal.
