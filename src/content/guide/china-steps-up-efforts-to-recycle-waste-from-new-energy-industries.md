China is tightening how waste from new energy industries is handled, and the rules that reach you depend on what your operation does and where it sits. This procedure is a way to find out which obligations apply before you commit to a site, a shipment or a contract. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so nothing here is priced or dated.

## Start from what your operation actually produces

Before you ask anyone about permits, write down the waste streams your operation will generate: spent cells, modules, electrolyte, separators, production scrap, packaging, and anything contaminated by those. Note whether each stream is solid, liquid or hazardous in your own understanding, and note the volume you expect per production cycle rather than per year.
This list is the document every official, landlord and contractor will ask for first. Keep it in Chinese and English, and update it whenever you change a process or a supplier.

## Ask which authority handles your site

Recycling obligations for new energy waste are administered at more than one level, and the office that handles your site depends on the industrial park, the district and the province. Ask your park management office or local ecology and environment bureau which office receives waste-related filings for your address, and ask for the name of the desk rather than a general hotline.
Do not assume that a rule described for another province applies to yours. Ask the receiving office to confirm in writing which categories of new energy waste they treat as hazardous and which as general industrial solid waste.

## Check the counterparty before you sign

If you will hand waste to a recycler or processor, ask for their registration and their permission to handle the specific waste category you produce. Ask to see the document that covers the category, not a general business licence.
Ask where the material physically goes after it leaves your site, and ask for the name of the receiving facility. If the answer is vague, treat that as a reason to keep looking.

## Build the paperwork into your move plan

Waste handling obligations attach to the site and to the entity that holds the permit, so decide early whether your Chinese entity or a contracted third party will be the registered handler. This affects what you can sign and when you can start producing.
Keep your waste list, your counterparty documents and your correspondence with the local office in one file. When an inspector or a landlord asks, you produce the file rather than reconstructing it.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-18; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
