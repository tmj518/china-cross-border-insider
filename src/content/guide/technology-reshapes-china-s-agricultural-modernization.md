Farm technology in China changes what a farm needs from the people who run it, and that changes what you are agreeing to when you apply. This guide sets out the order of checks to make before you sign or pay anything. It contains no figures because none could be verified against a source, and it is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What the application actually commits you to

Ask the counterparty to state in writing what the application is for: a lease, a service contract, a training place, a supply agreement, or a job. The answer decides which of the later checks matter most.
Get the full set of documents before you fill anything in, including the parts that are only mentioned in passing. If a document is described but not shown, treat the application as incomplete.
Separate what you are asked to pay from what you are asked to promise. A promise to buy, to deliver, or to stay for a fixed period can cost more than a fee.

## Check the land and the operator

Ask who holds the land and for how long, and ask to see the document that shows it. Do not rely on a verbal description of the arrangement.
Ask who operates the technology and who is responsible when it fails. On a farm using automated equipment, the answer is often a company rather than the landholder.
If the application involves equipment you will run, ask for the operating instructions and the maintenance terms in the language you will actually work in.

## Check your own status before you apply

Confirm what your current permission to be in China allows you to do, and whether the activity in the application fits it. This is the point where people most often discover a problem too late.
If the application depends on your visa or residence permission, do not submit it until that question is settled with someone qualified to answer it.
Keep copies of everything you submit and everything you are told, with the date you were told it.

## Payment and exit

Ask what happens to money already paid if the application is refused, if you withdraw, or if the arrangement ends early. Get the answer in the contract, not in conversation.
Ask how you end the arrangement and what notice is required. An arrangement you cannot leave is a different decision from one you can.
Do not pay anything before you have the documents and the answers above in writing.
