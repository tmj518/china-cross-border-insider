A documentary about Shenzhen can shape where you look for housing or work before you have set foot in the city. This procedure is about using the film as a prompt for questions, not as a source of figures. Every number you act on should come from an official or current source you have checked yourself.

## What the film is useful for

Treat the documentary as a map of themes: districts, commute patterns, industry, housing pressure, and the pace of change. It shows you what people talk about, not what anything costs or how long anything takes.
Note the places and situations that recur. Those are the things to research before you arrive, because they are the things the film chose to show.

## What the film cannot tell you

A documentary is edited for story. It may be out of date, it may omit ordinary daily life, and it will not give you current rents, visa rules, or processing times.
Do not use anything you see in the film as a basis for a deposit, a contract, or an application. Confirm each point with the relevant office or a current official page.

## Turning the film into a checklist

After watching, write down the questions it raised: which district, which commute, which kind of employer, which kind of housing. Keep the list short and specific.
For each question, decide where the answer must come from: an official website, a landlord or agent, an employer, or someone already living there. The film only tells you what to ask.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-18; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
