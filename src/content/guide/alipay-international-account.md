An Alipay account tied to your foreign identity lets you pay Chinese suppliers through a channel they already use. The setup is done inside the app, and most of the work is preparing documents and confirming what your supplier can actually receive. Do the checks below before you send anything.

## What the account is for

An Alipay international account is a consumer wallet opened with a non-Chinese passport and verified through identity checks in the app. It is built for paying people and businesses, not for holding business funds or running a company treasury.
For importers, the practical use is settling with a supplier who accepts Alipay, or paying a sourcing agent or sample fee where a bank transfer is slow or costly. Treat it as a payment rail, not a bank account.

## Documents to have ready

You will be asked to photograph your passport and take a live face check in the app. Have the passport in hand, not a scan, and use a phone with a working camera and a stable connection.
Have your supplier's exact registered name and the account details they want paid to, in writing. A mismatch between the name you type and the name on their account is the most common reason a payment stalls.

## Limits and what they mean for a purchase

Alipay applies limits to unverified and newly verified accounts, and the limits shown in your app are the ones that apply to you. Check them in the app before you agree a payment plan with a supplier.
If a single purchase is larger than your limit, split the arrangement or use a bank transfer instead. Do not promise a supplier a same-day Alipay payment until you have seen your own limit screen.

## Records and disputes

Keep the in-app payment record, the supplier's written confirmation of receipt, and your commercial invoice together. Alipay's dispute process works from the transaction record, so the record is your evidence.
If a payment is disputed, respond inside the app using the transaction reference. Do not rely on chat messages outside the platform as your main proof.
