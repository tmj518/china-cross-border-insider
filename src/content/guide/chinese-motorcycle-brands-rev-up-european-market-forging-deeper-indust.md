Chinese motorcycle brands are expanding in Europe and forming industrial ties with European firms. If you are relocating and considering one of these machines, or work that depends on them, check the claims yourself before you commit. This procedure lists the checks to perform in order. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure in this topic could be verified against a source.

## Why this matters for a relocating reader

A motorcycle you buy or import after a move ties up money, registration effort and, if something goes wrong, time you may not have. Brand claims about European market presence and industrial partnerships are marketing until you confirm them.
Treat every statement about a brand's European ties as unverified until you have seen it in a document you can keep. The checks below are ordered so that the cheapest, fastest checks come first.

## What you can and cannot confirm

You can confirm what a brand publishes, what a dealer will put in writing, and what a registration authority tells you directly. You cannot confirm market share, sales volumes or the depth of an industrial partnership from a press release alone.
Where a figure would normally settle a question, ask the source for the figure in writing instead of accepting a spoken claim. If no source will put it in writing, record that as a gap and decide accordingly.

## Where the risk sits

The risk is not the brand name. It is the gap between what you were told and what the paperwork says when you try to register, insure or service the machine where you will live.
Parts availability, warranty terms and service coverage are the points where an unverified claim costs you money. Check them before you pay, not after.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
