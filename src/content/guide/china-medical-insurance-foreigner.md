Two different things get called health cover in China, and confusing them costs money. One is the statutory employee scheme tied to social insurance registration. The other is a commercial policy an employer buys. This guide sets out the order to check them in. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## Two things, not one

The statutory employee scheme is the social insurance medical cover that comes with formal employment. It is tied to social insurance registration, which is handled through the employer and the local authorities where the workplace sits.
A commercial policy is a private contract, usually arranged by the employer, that pays toward treatment the statutory scheme does not cover. The two are not substitutes. One does not replace the other, and having one tells you nothing about whether you have the other.

## The statutory scheme follows the city

Rules for the statutory scheme are set locally. What is covered, which providers you can use, and how reimbursement works differ from city to city, and they can change.
Because of that, no figure in this guide would be reliable across cities, so none is given. Ask your employer which city your social insurance registration sits in, and get the current local rules from that city's social insurance office rather than from a colleague who worked elsewhere.

## What the commercial policy is for

A commercial policy is what covers treatment outside the statutory scheme: care the scheme does not include, providers the scheme does not list, and costs above what the scheme pays.
Read the policy as a contract, not as a benefit. Check what it excludes, which providers it recognises, whether it pays the hospital directly or reimburses you afterward, and what happens to cover if your employment ends.

## Status decides, not nationality

Which cover applies depends on your employment status. A foreigner on a local employment contract with social insurance registration is in a different position from a foreigner seconded from an overseas employer, posted short-term, or working under another arrangement.
Nationality alone does not settle it. Two people from the same country working in the same city can be covered differently because their contracts and registrations differ. Establish your status first, then ask which scheme and which policy attach to it.
