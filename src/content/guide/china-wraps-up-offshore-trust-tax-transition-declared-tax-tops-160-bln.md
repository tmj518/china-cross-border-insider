China has wrapped up a transition period for taxing offshore trusts, and declared tax on the topic is reported in the hundreds of billions of yuan. If you are relocating and a trust sits anywhere in your family's structure, the work is to find out whether it touches your Chinese position before you commit money. This is a procedure, not legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure here has been verified against a source.

## What the change means for you

The transition is described as finished, which means the softer treatment available while it ran is no longer the assumption to plan on. The declared total is large, which tells you the authorities are actively collecting under the new treatment rather than waiting.
What it does not tell you is whether your trust, your role in it, or your share of it falls inside the rules. That depends on facts about you and the trust that no general guide can settle.

## Get your facts together first

Before you speak to anyone paid by the hour, assemble the documents: the trust deed and any amendments, the names of trustees and beneficiaries, the dates of settlement and of any distributions, and your own residency history.
Write down what you actually receive from the trust and when, separately from what you are entitled to. Advisers will ask for this distinction and it changes the answer.

## Ask the right professional

You need someone who works on Chinese tax for individuals with cross-border structures, not a general accountant and not the trustee alone. The trustee's interest and yours are not the same.
Ask directly whether they have handled the transition rules since they were finalised, and ask them to put their view in writing.

## Before you move money

Do not restructure, distribute or transfer anything on the strength of what you read online, including this page. A step taken to fix a perceived problem can create a worse one.
If a deadline or filing date applies to you, your adviser should give it to you in writing with the rule it comes from.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-13; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
