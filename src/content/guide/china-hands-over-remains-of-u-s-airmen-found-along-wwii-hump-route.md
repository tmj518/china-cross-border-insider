If your family is relocating to China and you carry a question about an airman lost on the Hump Route, the handover of remains is handled by governments, not by individuals on the ground. Your part is to establish the record, route your request to the right office, and keep your contact details current as you move. This is a practical sequence, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## Who actually handles a recovery

Recovery and identification of U.S. service members from the Second World War in China is a government-to-government matter. The U.S. side works through its defense accounting agency for missing personnel; the Chinese side works through its foreign affairs and military channels. Neither side takes instructions from a private individual.
What a family can do is make sure the case file is complete and that the family's designated point of contact is reachable. That is the lever you have, and it matters most when you are about to change address and phone number.

## Before you leave for China

Gather what you already hold: service records, casualty reports, letters, photographs, and any previous correspondence about the case. Note the airman's full name, service branch, and the circumstances as your family recorded them.
Contact the U.S. defense accounting agency that handles missing WWII personnel and ask for the current status of the case and the name of the office of record. Ask what they need from you and in what form. Do this before you relocate, while you still have your home-country paperwork and phone access.

## Once you are in China

Do not approach recovery sites, wreck sites, or local officials on your own to press a case. Site access and any excavation are controlled by the Chinese authorities, and an unsolicited approach can complicate the official channel.
If you are asked to provide documents from inside China, use the office of record you identified earlier and follow its instructions on certification and translation. Keep copies of everything you send.

## Keeping the case alive while you move

Update your contact details with the office of record whenever your address, phone number, or email changes. A case that cannot reach the family stalls.
Keep a single file, in one place, with the case reference, the name of the office of record, and copies of every letter and form. If a relative in your home country is the family point of contact, agree in writing who speaks for the family and tell the office of record.
