This guide sets out the order in which to handle a move from Beijing to Shenzhen. It covers what to confirm before you spend money on an application, and what to keep in writing. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so treat every number you are quoted as something to confirm yourself.

## What this guide does and does not cover

The topic behind this guide is China's push for a more connected, dynamic Asia-Pacific, and what that means for someone relocating. The practical effect for a reader is that Shenzhen sits inside a region where cross-border work, travel and business links are being promoted, so the paperwork you are asked for may differ from what you handled in Beijing.
This guide gives you the order of operations only. It does not price your move, does not tell you which permit or visa you qualify for, and does not replace advice from a qualified professional or from the authority that issues your document.
Because the fact sheet for this topic is empty, no figure in this guide has been checked against a source. Where a step would normally carry a number, the step describes the timing in words instead. Confirm every amount, deadline and count with the issuing office before you rely on it.

## Before you commit money

Do the confirmation work first and the payment second. Most of the cost of a relocation is hard to recover once it is spent, and an application fee is usually the least recoverable of all.
Ask the receiving office, not a forum or an agent's brochure, what the current requirements are. Requirements in this area change, and a document that was correct for a colleague may not be correct for you.
Keep a written record of who told you what and when. If a requirement later turns out to be wrong, that record is what you will use to sort it out.

## What to keep in writing

Keep copies of every form you submit, every receipt, and every message from an agent or employer about your application. Store them somewhere you can reach after you have left Beijing.
If an employer or relocation agent is handling part of the process, get their scope in writing: which steps they perform, which steps remain yours, and who pays each fee. Do not assume the boundary is obvious.
Do not hand over original documents unless you have a receipt for them and know when they will come back.

## After you arrive

The procedure does not end at the border. Registration, housing paperwork and your employer's own onboarding steps usually follow arrival, and each has its own office and its own queue.
Complete the arrival-side steps in the order the local office gives you, and keep the receipts with the rest of your file. If a step depends on a document you have not received yet, wait for the document rather than submitting a substitute.
