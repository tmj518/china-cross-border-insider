You are moving to Papua New Guinea and have seen a headline about stronger China–Papua New Guinea ties. That headline is about diplomacy between governments, not about your visa, your work permit or your costs. This guide gives you the order of steps to take, and it deliberately contains no numbers because none could be verified against a source.

## What the headline does and does not change

A statement that ties between two countries will be boosted is a political statement. It does not by itself change any immigration rule, fee or processing time that applies to you.
Treat any claim that the statement changes your application as unverified until you see it on an official government page or in writing from the office handling your case.
This is not legal, tax or immigration advice. Confirm every requirement with the relevant authority before you pay or travel.

## Confirm which authority handles your case

Before you fill in anything, find out which body issues the permission you need and which office you submit to. Do this from the official source, not from an agent's summary.
Write down the name of the office, the exact permission type and the channel for submitting it. Keep that record with your other documents.

## Check the current requirements and costs at the source

Requirements and fees change. Read the current published requirements on the official page on the day you prepare your file, and read them again before you submit.
If a figure you were given cannot be found on an official page, do not rely on it and do not repeat it to anyone as fact.

## Keep your own record

Keep copies of every form, receipt and message. Note who told you what and when, so you can point to the source if a requirement is disputed later.
If an agent or adviser gives you a number that is not on an official page, ask them to show you where it comes from in writing.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-17; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
