A fatal apartment fire in Taiyuan is a reminder that the building you rent in matters as much as the flat. This procedure walks you through what to check and ask before you sign, using only actions you can take yourself. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice, and no figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so nothing here quotes one.

## Why this belongs in your relocation checklist

When you are moving to a new city, the lease decision usually gets made fast and on price and commute. Fire safety is rarely on the list because nobody hands you a checklist for it. The Taiyuan fire is the kind of event that makes the gap visible.
You do not need technical training to spot the basics. You need to look at the building with your own eyes, ask the landlord direct questions, and write down what you were told. That record is what protects you if something later turns out to be wrong.

## What to inspect in the building

Walk the route from the flat door to the outside of the building, the way you would in an emergency, and do it in daylight and again after dark. Note whether the stairs are clear, whether doors along the route open freely, and whether anything is stored in the corridors.
Look at the flat itself for smoke detection, for a way out that does not depend on the lift, and for anything that would block a window or a balcony. If you cannot tell whether a detector works, ask the landlord to show you.

## What to ask the landlord and the building management

Ask who is responsible for maintaining fire equipment in the building and how often it is checked. Ask what you are expected to do as a tenant and what the building does. Ask for the answers in writing, even a message thread is enough.
If the landlord cannot answer, ask building management directly. If neither can, treat that as information about the building and weigh it in your decision.

## What to record before you sign

Photograph what you saw: the stairwell, the corridor, the detector, the exit route. Keep the photos with your lease paperwork. Write a short note of what the landlord told you and the date you were told it.
If you later find something that was misrepresented, having your own record makes the conversation easier. This is not a substitute for advice from a qualified professional, and it does not guarantee any outcome.
