This guide covers the order in which you assemble and file a production permit application after you arrive. It describes the sequence only. No figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so nothing here carries a number, and you should confirm every threshold and deadline with the issuing office before you act.

## What this guide does and does not cover

This is a procedural walkthrough for one application. It tells you what to do and in what order. It does not tell you whether you qualify, what the fee is, or how long the office will take.
Nothing here is legal, tax or immigration advice. The rules differ by locality and by the type of production involved, and they change. Treat the steps as a checklist to bring to the counter, not as a substitute for the counter.

## Before you file anything

Confirm which office has jurisdiction over your premises. The answer depends on where the production site sits, not where you live, and getting this wrong means starting over.
Ask that office for its current written list of required documents. Ask for it in writing so you can match your file against it item by item. Do not rely on a list a colleague filed under.

## The order that saves you a second trip

Most rejections come from filing documents out of sequence, not from missing qualifications. Establish your legal presence first, then the site, then the production-specific approvals.
Keep a copy of everything you submit, stamped with the date it was received. That receipt is what you show when a later office asks whether an earlier step was completed.

## After you submit

Ask for the name of the officer handling your file and a way to reach them. Follow up in writing rather than in person where the office allows it, so you have a record.
If the office requests additional documents, supply them before any stated deadline and keep proof of when you did. If you cannot meet a deadline, tell the officer before it passes rather than after.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-19; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
