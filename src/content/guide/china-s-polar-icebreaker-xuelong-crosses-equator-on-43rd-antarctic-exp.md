News about the icebreaker Xuelong crossing the equator on an Antarctic expedition tells you nothing about your own relocation. This guide gives you a procedure for checking what a headline actually covers before you act on it. It is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What the headline covers

The Xuelong story is about a research vessel and its Antarctic voyage. It is not a notice about visas, residence permits, customs or shipping for private individuals.
Treat any polar expedition item as background reading. Do not use it to plan a move, a shipment or an application.

## Where your relocation figures come from

The fact sheet supplied for this topic is empty, so no figure in this guide is verified. Do not fill the gap with numbers from memory or from other articles.
Get every date, fee and deadline from the authority that issues the document you need, or from the office that will receive your application. Ask them directly and keep the reply.

## What to do with a news item you cannot verify

If a story has no source you can check, set it aside. Note what you would need to confirm and who could confirm it.
Do not repeat an unverified figure to a landlord, employer or shipping agent. A wrong number costs you money or time.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-10; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
