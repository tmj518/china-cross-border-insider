Summit media accreditation is handled by the event organisers, not by a visa office, and the rules change with each event. This guide sets out the order of work for a delegate or reporter applying from abroad. It contains no dates, fees or quotas because none could be verified against a source.

## Find the official accreditation channel first

Start from the summit's own website or the press office of the body hosting it. Do not rely on forwarded emails, chat groups or third-party agents who offer to 'handle' accreditation for a fee.
Confirm in writing which office receives applications and which address it accepts them from. Keep that confirmation.

## Check what your entry document requires

Accreditation and a visa are separate processes. Approval as media does not by itself grant entry, and a visa does not guarantee a press pass.
Ask the Chinese embassy or consulate serving your location which visa category applies to your purpose and what supporting letter they expect. Rules differ by nationality and by purpose, so get the answer for your own case.

## Assemble the documents organisers usually ask for

Typical requests include a letter from your employer or outlet naming you and your role, a copy of your passport, a photograph to the specified format, and evidence of previous published work.
Prepare these before the application window opens. Replace any document that will expire before your trip ends.

## When no figures are published

If the organiser has not stated a deadline, a fee or a processing time, do not assume one. Ask the press office directly and keep the reply.
Submit as early as the channel allows, and treat any silence as a reason to follow up rather than to wait.

## After you submit

Record what you sent, when, and to whom. If you are asked for more documents, send exactly what is requested and nothing extra.
Do not travel on the assumption of approval. Confirm the outcome in writing before you book anything that cannot be cancelled.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
