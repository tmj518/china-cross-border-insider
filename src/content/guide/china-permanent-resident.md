Permanent residence in China is applied for through the public security exit-entry administration, usually where you live. The order below is the order that matters: confirm your category first, because everything you gather depends on it. This is a description of the procedure, not legal, tax or immigration advice, and it contains no figures because none could be verified against a source.

## Confirm which category you are applying under

Permanent residence is granted under defined categories, and each category has its own evidence requirements. The categories are set out in the governing rules, and the local exit-entry administration applies them. Ask the window which category your situation falls under before you collect anything, because the document list changes with the category.
Do not assume that a category you have read about online is the one that applies where you live. Local practice on accepting and checking documents varies by city, and the office that receives your file is the one whose list you should follow.

## Get the local document list in writing

Ask the exit-entry administration for the current checklist for your category and keep the copy they give you. The list tells you which documents need notarisation, which need translation, and which need to be issued by an authority outside China.
Documents issued abroad usually need to be authenticated or legalised before they are accepted in China, and translations are often required to be done by an accepted translator. Confirm the current requirement at the window rather than relying on an older list.

## Assemble and check the file before you submit

Collect the documents on the checklist and check names, dates and document numbers against your passport and against each other. Mismatches between a name on a foreign document and a name on a Chinese document are a common reason a file is sent back.
Keep the originals and bring copies as the checklist requires. If a document has an expiry, make sure it will still be valid when you submit, and note that some supporting documents must be issued within a set period before submission, so ask the window how fresh they need to be.

## Submit, attend the interview, and track the decision

Submit in person at the office that handles your area, unless they tell you otherwise, and keep the receipt they issue. You may be asked to attend an interview or to provide further documents, and the file can be paused until you do.
Processing takes time and the office will tell you how to check on the file. Do not plan travel or give up your current residence permit on the assumption of a date; keep your existing status valid while the application is pending.

## After a decision

If the application is approved, follow the office's instructions on collecting the document and on registering your residence. If it is refused, ask for the reason in writing and ask whether you can reapply or correct the file.
Keep copies of everything you submitted and everything you received. You will need them if you later apply for a replacement, update your details, or deal with a bank, employer or landlord who asks for proof of your status.
