Health cooperation between China and African countries is announced often, but announcements are not the same as programmes you can use. Before you let a headline about health ties shape where you move or what you pay for, verify the specific arrangement yourself. This guide lists the checks to run, in order, and what to do when a claim cannot be verified.

## Why this matters for a move

Health cooperation can affect you in concrete ways: where training places exist, which hospitals host exchange programmes, and whether a qualification you hold is recognised at your destination. None of that follows automatically from a signed agreement between governments.
Treat any health-ties claim as a lead to check, not a fact to plan around. If you cannot find the programme on an official source, plan as if it does not exist for you.

## What you can and cannot confirm

You can usually confirm whether a cooperation framework is mentioned on the websites of the health ministry or foreign ministry of the countries involved, or on the site of the Chinese embassy in the African country concerned.
You generally cannot confirm, from public material alone, how many places a programme offers, when intake opens, what it costs, or whether it applies to foreign residents rather than nationals. Do not assume any of these in your favour.

## When a claim does not check out

If a programme cannot be found on an official source, do not pay any fee, deposit or agent charge connected to it. Ask the institution named in the claim, in writing, whether the programme exists and who is eligible.
Keep the reply. If you later deal with an agent or recruiter, that written answer is your reference point.

## Limits of this guide

This is a set of verification steps, not legal, tax or immigration advice. It does not tell you whether you qualify for anything. Eligibility and recognition questions go to the relevant ministry, embassy or licensing body in writing.
