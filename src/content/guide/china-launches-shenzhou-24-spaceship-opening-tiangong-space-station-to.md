China launched the Shenzhou-24 spaceship, and Tiangong is opening to its first foreign astronaut. If you are relocating, this is a story to follow carefully rather than act on. The steps below are about checking what is actually announced before you change any plan or spend any money.

## What has been announced

The launch of Shenzhou-24 and the arrival of a foreign astronaut at Tiangong are reported as mission events. They are not, by themselves, a change to visa rules, residency permits or hiring requirements.
Treat mission news as context. Anything that affects your move will be published separately by the body that handles it.

## What this does not change

A space mission does not alter the documents you need to live and work in China. Your permit, your employer's paperwork and your registration steps stay as they are.
Do not delay or speed up an application because of a launch. Follow the instructions on your own paperwork.

## Where to check

Use the official channel that issued your document, not a news report about the mission. If a rule changes, it will appear there first.
If you are considering a role connected to the space programme, ask the employer which body handles the permit and confirm the requirements in writing.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-28; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
