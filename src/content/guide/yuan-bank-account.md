This guide walks you through opening a yuan bank account for your importing business. It covers documentation, bank selection, and ongoing requirements. It is not legal, tax, or immigration advice.

## Before You Apply

You need your business license, passport, and proof of address in China. Some banks also ask for your import registration or customs code.
Choose a bank that handles foreign trade regularly. Ask about fees for receiving and sending yuan, and whether they support online banking in English.

## The Application Process

Visit the bank branch in person. Bring original documents and copies. The bank will verify your identity and business status.
You will fill out an application form and sign agreements. The bank may take time to review before approving.

## After Approval

You receive your account number and a bank card or passbook. Set up online banking and mobile alerts.
Keep your business license and passport copies updated with the bank. Report any changes to your business registration.

## Using the Account for Imports

For each import payment, you may need to provide the contract, invoice, and customs declaration. The bank will check the transaction against your import records.
Some payments require approval from the foreign exchange authority. Plan ahead for these checks.

## Maintaining the Account

Review your account activity regularly. Some banks require you to visit the branch periodically to keep the account active.
If you stop using the account, close it properly to avoid fees or restrictions.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
