APEC commitments are government-level statements, not a personal visa or permit. Before you rely on anything you have read about China's APEC role, work out which part of your move it could actually touch, then confirm that part with the office that issues the document. This procedure is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What APEC does and does not decide for you

APEC is a forum where economies discuss trade and cooperation. Its statements describe intentions between governments. They do not create an individual right to enter, work or stay, and they do not set the fee or processing time for your application.
Your relocation is governed by the Chinese rules that apply to your situation and by the documents the issuing office asks you to provide. Treat any APEC reference in an article as background, not as a rule you can cite at a counter.

## Find the office that actually handles your case

Identify the specific office that will receive your application: the entry and exit administration, the local labour or foreign expert office, or your employer's designated handler. Write down its name and where it sits.
Ask that office which category you fall under and what it requires from you. Do not ask whether APEC applies; ask what your category requires. The answer is the only one that matters for your file.

## Check every figure against the issuing office

Fees, processing times and validity periods change and differ by city and category. Get each figure from the office that will process your file, in writing if you can, and note who told you and when.
If a number appears in an article about APEC or about China's opening to the world, do not carry it into your planning. Replace it with the figure the office gives you, or plan without a figure until you have one.

## Keep your file consistent

The name, purpose and dates on your application should match your supporting documents and your employer's paperwork. Inconsistency is the most common reason a file is sent back.
Keep copies of everything you submit and note the date you submitted it. When you follow up, refer to that date and the office you dealt with.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-20; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
