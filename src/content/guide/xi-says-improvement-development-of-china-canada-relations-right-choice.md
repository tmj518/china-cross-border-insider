Statements about the state of China-Canada relations do not change your permit, your documents or your costs. This procedure tells you what to verify yourself before you commit money or time. It contains no figures because none could be verified against a source.

## What a diplomatic statement does and does not do

A statement that improved relations are the right choice is a political position. It does not amend visa rules, residence requirements or document checklists. Treat it as context, not as an instruction.
Your application is decided under the rules in force when you apply. Those rules are published by the authority that handles your application, not by news reports.

## Where to get the rules that bind you

Go to the official website of the Chinese embassy or consulate covering your place of residence. Read the visa and residence sections there.
If you are applying for work or study, also read the pages of the Chinese government body that regulates that category. Confirm which office receives your application and which documents it lists.

## What to confirm before you pay anything

Confirm the current document checklist, the fee, the processing route and the validity period of the permit you are seeking. Ask the receiving office directly if a page is unclear or looks out of date.
Keep a copy of the page you relied on and the date you read it. If a rule changes between your check and your submission, the copy shows what you were working from.

## What this guide does not cover

This is not legal, tax or immigration advice. It does not tell you whether you qualify for any category.
No figure in this topic could be verified against a source, so none is given. Get every number you need from the office handling your application.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-17; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
