A Z visa is the work visa you apply for from outside China, and it is only one part of the process. The employer starts it, you finish it at a Chinese embassy or consulate, and the permit that lets you stay is issued after you arrive. This is the order of steps, not legal or immigration advice.

## Who starts the process

Your employer in China applies for the work permit notice on your behalf. You cannot start this yourself. Until that notice is issued, there is nothing for you to apply for.
Ask your employer which documents they need from you and in what form. Requirements differ by city and by employer, so treat their list as the one that matters.

## The application outside China

With the notice in hand, you apply for the Z visa at the Chinese embassy or consulate responsible for where you live. Book an appointment if the mission requires one, and check its own page for what it accepts.
Bring the notice, your passport, photographs and the forms the mission lists. The visa is usually single entry, so plan your travel around that.

## After you land

The Z visa lets you enter, not stay. Within the window the visa allows, you and your employer convert it into a residence permit at the local exit-entry administration.
The residence permit is the document that lets you live and work in China. Do not let the entry visa lapse before the permit is issued.

## What to keep in mind

No figure in this guide is verified, so confirm every deadline with your employer and the mission handling your case. Do not rely on a number you read elsewhere.
Keep copies of every document you submit. You will need them again at the residence permit stage.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
