A Chinese vice-president attending a climate summit in Antalya is diplomacy, not a change to your visa, permit or tax position. Treat the headline as background and work from the requirements your own application lists. This guide sets out the order to check things in so a news cycle does not cost you time or money.

## What the summit does and does not affect

A leaders' summit produces statements and commitments between governments. It does not amend the rules that govern your residence permit, work permit or tax registration.
If a summit produces an agreement, the change reaches you only after your host country and China adopt it through their own procedures. That is a separate process with its own timeline, and it will be published by the authorities that issue your documents.
Nothing in a summit report tells you what to file, when to file it or what it costs. Your application checklist does.

## Check your own application first

Open the checklist issued by the authority handling your application. Confirm the document list, the validity period of each document and the address where you submit.
Compare the checklist against what you already hold. Note anything missing or close to expiring.
If a document is close to expiry, renew it before you submit rather than after. A document that lapses mid-application can force you to start again.

## Verify anything you read about policy change

When you see a claim that a summit changed the rules, find the primary notice from the authority that issues your document. If no such notice exists, treat the claim as unconfirmed.
Do not pay an agent, a consultant or a translation service on the strength of a news report alone. Ask them to point to the notice they are relying on.
Keep copies of the notices you check, with the date you checked them, so you can show what your application was based on.

## Keep the two tracks separate

Track one is your relocation file: documents, submissions, appointments and payments. Track two is news about international meetings. Only track one has deadlines attached to your name.
When a headline makes you anxious about your file, go back to the checklist and confirm the next action. That is the only thing that changes your position.
This guide is not legal, tax or immigration advice. Confirm your own requirements with the issuing authority or a qualified professional.
