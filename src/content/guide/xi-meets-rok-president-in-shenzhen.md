The meeting between China's president and the Republic of Korea's president in Shenzhen is a high-level diplomatic event. If you plan to attend or be nearby, expect strict security and logistical changes. This guide outlines what to do before, during, and after the event.

## Before the Event

Check official announcements from the Chinese Foreign Ministry and the ROK embassy for any public access details. Most bilateral summits are closed to the public, so assume you cannot enter the venue.
If you have a legitimate reason to be near the venue (e.g., work, residence), carry your passport and residence permit at all times. Police may set up checkpoints and ask for identification.

## During the Event

Expect road closures and traffic diversions around the meeting location. Use public transport or plan extra travel time if you must move through the area.
Do not attempt to approach the venue or the motorcade. Security personnel will enforce a buffer zone, and any violation may lead to detention or questioning.

## After the Event

Once the meeting concludes, normal access usually resumes. However, stay alert for any residual security measures or announcements about follow-up events.
If you were detained or questioned, contact your embassy or consulate for assistance. Keep a record of the incident and any documents provided by authorities.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-19; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
