## Definition

The China student visa is the entry permission a foreign national applies for in order to enrol in a Chinese educational institution, and this article describes the procedure an applicant works through rather than the price of it.

## What the process is for

A student visa is not issued on the strength of an admission letter alone. It is the end of a chain: an institution accepts the applicant, the applicant uses that acceptance to obtain a document from the Chinese authorities, and the visa is then issued against that document. Each link in the chain is issued by a different body, and an application fails when the applicant treats the chain as a single step.

## Who qualifies

The applicant must have been accepted for study by a recognised Chinese educational institution. The institution is the party that initiates the process, because the document the visa application rests on is issued to the institution's students and not to applicants at large. An applicant who has not been accepted has nothing to apply with.

## Steps

The sequence matters, and it is the part most often got wrong:

1. Apply to the educational institution and receive its acceptance.
2. The institution obtains the study-related document on the applicant's behalf from the competent Chinese authority.
3. Assemble the visa application around that document, using the receiving mission's own published list.
4. Confirm which embassy or consulate has jurisdiction over the applicant's place of residence.
5. Submit the visa application to that mission, with the passport, form, photograph and supporting papers.
6. Receive the visa and check that its validity covers the intended entry.
7. Enter China within the validity of that visa and complete enrolment at the institution.

The document from step two is the pivot of the whole process. It cannot be obtained before acceptance, and the visa cannot be applied for before it is issued.

## Documents the application is built from

The application file is assembled from the applicant's own papers plus the institution's document. In practice the file covers:

- the applicant's passport, valid for longer than the intended stay;
- the visa application form, completed and signed;
- a recent photograph to the specified format;
- the study-related document issued through the institution;
- proof of the applicant's financial capacity to cover study and living costs;
- the institution's admission or acceptance letter;
- supporting papers for any accompanying family members.

The exact list is set by the mission that receives the application, and the mission's own published list governs. An applicant who works from a third-party checklist rather than the receiving mission's list is the most common source of a returned file.

## Conditions that block an application

An application is refused or returned when the chain is incomplete rather than when the applicant is otherwise unsuitable. The recurring blockers are:

- a passport whose remaining validity does not cover the intended stay;
- a study document that does not match the institution named in the admission letter;
- financial evidence that does not cover the full period of study;
- an application filed at a mission that does not have jurisdiction over the applicant's place of residence;
- a form that is incomplete, unsigned, or carries a photograph that does not meet the format.

## Why applications are sent back

Files come back for reasons that are procedural, not substantive. The document set is inconsistent between the institution's papers and the applicant's own. The financial evidence covers a shorter period than the study. The application is filed at the wrong mission. The photograph or the form does not meet the format the receiving mission publishes. None of these is a judgement on the applicant; each is a mismatch between what was submitted and what the receiving mission's list asks for.

## What this article does not price

The fee for the visa itself is set by the mission that receives the application, and the published fee schedules are not uniform across missions. This article therefore states no amount. An applicant should read the fee schedule published by the specific embassy or consulate that will receive the file, because that is the only figure that applies to that application.

## How confident

- **What we know:** the process runs institution first, then the study-related document, then the visa application at the mission with jurisdiction; the file is built from the applicant's passport, form, photograph, the institution's document, financial evidence and the admission letter; and files are returned for mismatches against the receiving mission's published list.
- **What we do not know:** the fee, the processing time, and the validity period granted. The official pages reached in this run did not publish these, and no figure is stated here rather than an estimate being supplied.
- **How to check:** the fee schedule and document list published by the Chinese embassy or consulate that will receive the application, and the admissions office of the accepting institution, which issues the study-related document.
