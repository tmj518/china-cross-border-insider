Alipay is the payment app most people in China use for everyday purchases, and you can prepare it before you move. Do the setup while you still have your home phone number and bank cards, because changing either after you arrive makes verification harder. This is a practical checklist, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What you need before you start

You need a smartphone that will work in China, your current mobile number that can receive SMS, your passport, and a bank card issued in your home country. Keep the phone number active until you have finished setup and added a payment method.
Decide which card you will use for payments. A card that charges foreign transaction fees will cost you more on every purchase, so check your card terms before you link it.

## Register with the number you have now

Install Alipay from your phone's official app store and open it. Choose to register with your current mobile number and enter the code the app sends you. Do not use a number you plan to cancel before you leave, because you will need it to log in and to confirm changes.
If you already have a Chinese phone number, you can use it, but you do not need one to start. The app accepts foreign numbers for registration.

## Verify your identity with your passport

Open the identity verification section and follow the prompts to scan your passport and take a photo of yourself. Use the passport you will travel on, and make sure the photo page is clear and not covered by glare.
Verification can take time to complete. Do it well before your departure date so you are not waiting on it while you pack.

## Add a payment method and test it

Add your bank card in the payment methods section and complete any confirmation your bank requires. Then make a small test payment, such as a top-up or a purchase, to confirm the card works with the app.
Keep a second card or a small amount of cash as a backup. Some foreign cards are declined by some merchants, and you want a fallback on your first days in China.
