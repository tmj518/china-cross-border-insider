APEC-year momentum has made China's open-market events more visible, and that attention can help or mislead a new arrival. This procedure sets out the order of actions for turning that momentum into a practical plan. It contains no numbers because none could be verified, and it is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What the momentum actually changes for you

APEC-year attention raises the profile of Asia-Pacific trade and of China's import exhibitions. For a relocating reader, the practical effect is more meetings, more delegations and more competing claims about market access.
Treat the attention as a scheduling signal, not as evidence of any specific rule, fee or timeline. Confirm every operational detail with the authority or counterparty that owns it.

## Decide what you are applying for before you apply

Separate the exhibition presence from the right to live and work in China. They are different applications with different offices and different evidence.
Write down which one you are pursuing first, because the paperwork for one does not substitute for the other.

## Build the file from primary sources

Collect documents from the issuing body rather than from intermediaries who cite the APEC-year mood. Keep copies of everything you submit and note who received it.
If a figure appears in a brochure or a contact's message, ask for the source document before you rely on it.

## Keep the plan reversible

Do not commit money to a venue, a lease or a shipment before the application you depend on has a written outcome. Sequence commitments so that a refusal leaves you able to withdraw.
Review the plan whenever an official notice changes, and keep the previous version on file.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-09; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
