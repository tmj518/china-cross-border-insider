Former deserted mining sites in China are being transformed into film sets and tourist attractions. If you are relocating to one of these areas, this guide outlines the steps to settle in and navigate the new environment.

## Understanding the Local Situation

These sites are often in remote areas with limited infrastructure. Services such as banking, healthcare, and international schools may be scarce or require travel to nearby towns.
The local economy is shifting from mining to film production and tourism. This means job opportunities may be seasonal or project-based, and housing options could be temporary or limited.

## Legal and Administrative Steps

Ensure your visa and residence permit are valid for the area. Some sites are in special zones with additional regulations.
Register your address with the local police station within the required timeframe after moving. This is mandatory for all foreign residents.

## Finding Housing and Daily Necessities

Housing may be in converted mining dormitories or new developments near the film sets. Check the condition and availability of utilities like water, electricity, and internet before signing a lease.
For daily shopping, identify the nearest market or supermarket. In some areas, delivery services may be limited, so plan accordingly.

## Work and Community Integration

If you are moving for work, confirm your employment contract and understand the terms, including duration and housing provisions. Many jobs are tied to film production schedules.
Connect with local expat communities or film industry networks. They can provide practical advice and help you adapt to the local culture.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-12; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
