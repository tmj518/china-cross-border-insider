China has extended its suspension of the additional tariff on certain U.S. goods. If you are relocating and shipping household goods or buying imported items, the tariff status of your goods affects what you pay at customs. This procedure covers how to check your own shipment; it is not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## What the extension does and does not cover

The extension keeps the suspension in place for a further period, but it applies only to the goods and classifications named in the official notice. It is not a blanket removal of tariffs on everything from the United States.
Goods outside the listed classifications remain subject to whatever tariff applies to them. Do not assume your shipment is covered because it is personal property or because it comes from the United States.

## Find the official notice before you rely on anything

The controlling documents are published by the authority that administers tariffs and by customs. Read the notice itself rather than news summaries, which often omit the product list and the effective dates.
Keep a copy of the notice you relied on, with the date you accessed it, in case a broker or customs officer questions your classification.

## Match your goods to the notice

The notice works by product classification, not by description in plain language. Your freight forwarder or customs broker can tell you the classification code that will be used for your shipment.
Compare that code against the list in the notice. If your code is not on the list, the suspension does not apply to your goods and you should budget for the tariff that does.

## Get the timing right for your move

The suspension runs for a limited period. What matters is the date your goods are declared to customs, not the date you bought them or the date they left the United States.
If your shipment is likely to arrive near the end of the suspension period, ask your broker what happens if the suspension lapses before your declaration. Plan for the possibility that the tariff applies.
