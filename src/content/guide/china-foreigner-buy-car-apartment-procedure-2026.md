## Steps

1. Confirm residence status. Before anything is signed, establish that the residence permit is valid and covers the city where the property sits or the vehicle will be plated. Both files inherit this permit's expiry date.
2. Confirm eligibility with the city authority. For property, obtain the housing authority's confirmation that the buyer may hold residential property in that city. For a vehicle, confirm with the traffic management office that the permit and licence support registration in the buyer's own name.
3. Assemble the personal documents. Passport, residence permit, proof of employment or study status, and — for property — a legalised or apostilled marital-status certificate with a translation.
4. Assemble the transaction documents. For property, the title certificate and the sale contract. For a vehicle, the purchase invoice, the certificate of conformity or import certificate, and the customs declaration where the vehicle was imported.
5. Satisfy the money and insurance conditions. For property, lodge and verify the funds trail showing the lawful source of the purchase money. For a vehicle, pay the purchase tax and take out the compulsory traffic insurance.
6. Register the transaction. For property, register the contract and then file the title transfer. For a vehicle, present it to the traffic management office for inspection and plating.
7. Keep the file current. Renew the residence permit before it expires, because the plate and the title both hang from it at the next inspection or transfer.

## Buying residential property: the shape of the file

A foreign national buying a home in mainland China is not treated as a walk-in cash buyer. The procedure is built around proving that the buyer is resident, that the money is accounted for, and that the property is the one the buyer will live in.

**Who the file is for.** The buyer must hold a valid residence status in China and must be purchasing for own occupation rather than for letting or resale. A buyer who is not resident, or who is buying on behalf of someone else, is outside the procedure rather than at the back of a queue.

**What the file contains.** Expect to produce, at minimum: a valid passport; a valid residence permit; proof of the buyer's employment or study status in the city where the property sits; a certificate of the buyer's marital status, legalised or apostilled in the country of issue and translated; and the funds trail showing where the purchase money came from. The property side supplies the title certificate and the sale contract. The city's housing authority supplies the eligibility confirmation that the buyer may hold residential property there.

**The order of the steps.** Eligibility confirmation comes first, before any contract is signed. A contract signed before eligibility is confirmed is the single most common reason a file is sent back, because the housing authority cannot confirm eligibility for a transaction that already exists. After eligibility, the funds are lodged and verified, then the contract is registered, then the title transfer is filed. Each stage consumes the output of the one before it.

**What blocks it.** A residence permit that does not cover the city of purchase. A buyer who already holds residential property in the same city, where the local rule limits a household to one home. Purchase money that cannot be traced to a lawful source. A property that is not cleared for sale to a non-resident buyer, including some categories of housing built for local residents only.

## Registering a car: the shape of the file

The vehicle question is asked as "can you buy a car in China as a foreigner", and the answer turns on registration rather than on the sale. A dealer will take the money; the vehicle cannot legally move until it is plated, and plating is where the foreign buyer's file is tested.

**Who the file is for.** The person registering the vehicle must hold a valid residence permit and, where the vehicle is to be driven, a Chinese driving licence or a recognised foreign licence converted to one. A buyer without residence status can own a vehicle in some circumstances but cannot register it for road use in their own name.

**What the file contains.** A valid passport and residence permit; the Chinese driving licence; the vehicle purchase invoice; the certificate of conformity or import certificate for the vehicle; the compulsory traffic insurance policy; and the vehicle purchase tax payment certificate. For an imported vehicle, the customs declaration and the import licence documents join the file.

**The order of the steps.** The vehicle is bought and invoiced, the purchase tax is paid, the compulsory insurance is taken out, and only then is the vehicle presented to the traffic management office for inspection and plating. The plate is issued against the residence permit, so a permit that expires shortly after purchase creates a renewal problem at the next inspection rather than at the first.

**What blocks it.** A residence permit that expires before the registration is completed. A vehicle whose certificate of conformity does not match the invoiced specification. An imported vehicle whose customs documents do not show lawful entry. A driving licence that has not been converted where conversion is required.

## What both procedures have in common

Neither file is refused for lack of money. Both are refused for a document that does not match the person, the place, or the sequence. The residence permit is the spine of both: it establishes that the buyer is in the system, it ties the buyer to a city, and it carries an expiry date that the rest of the file inherits. A buyer who sorts the permit first, and confirms eligibility before signing anything, meets the procedure in the order it is written.
