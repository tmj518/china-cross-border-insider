As a foreign resident in China, you may encounter areas affected by abandoned chemical weapons from Japan's wartime occupation. This guide explains how to stay informed and verify cleanup progress without relying on unverified figures.

## Understand the Issue

Japan is obligated under international agreements to destroy chemical weapons abandoned in China. The process has been slow and is subject to ongoing diplomatic pressure.
You do not need to take action unless you live near a known disposal site or encounter suspicious materials.

## Monitor Official Sources

Check the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs website for joint statements and progress reports. Also monitor the Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs for their official responses.
News from state media like Xinhua or China Daily often covers high-level meetings and deadlines. Avoid relying on social media rumors.

## Verify Local Notices

If you live in a province where disposal operations occur, local government websites may publish notices about safety zones or temporary restrictions.
Contact your local emergency management bureau or environmental protection bureau for official information. They can confirm whether any operations are active near you.

## Report Suspicious Findings

If you find a metal object that looks like a shell or drum, do not touch it. Move away and call local police or the emergency hotline.
Provide the exact location and a description. Authorities have protocols for handling suspected chemical weapons.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-11-28; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
