Your work permit application is assessed on a points system that classifies you into a category. That category determines what your employer must prove and how easy the approval is. This guide explains how the points work so you know what to expect.

## Categories and What They Mean

Foreign workers are classified into categories, and the category decides what the employer must prove. Higher categories require more documentation and stricter proof from your employer, while lower categories are simpler.
Your category is not something you negotiate with your employer. It is decided on the application based on the points you earn.

## What the Points Weigh

The classification weighs salary, education, age and work experience against each other. Each factor contributes points, and the total places you in a category.
No single factor guarantees a category. The points system balances strengths and weaknesses across all four areas.

## How Offsets Work

A higher salary can offset a weaker qualification, and the reverse is also true. For example, strong education or long experience can compensate for a lower salary offer.
This means you are not automatically disqualified if one factor is below average. The overall score is what matters.

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-10-28; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
