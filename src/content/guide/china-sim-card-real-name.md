In China a phone line is tied to a verified identity before it works, and for a foreigner that identity is your passport. The registration is what links the number to you, so the name on the line matters as much as the number itself. This is the order of steps to get a line in your own name.

## Why the name on the line matters

A line is registered to a verified identity before it is activated. The carrier checks the identity document at the counter and records the line against it. That record is the link between the number and the person holding it.
For a foreigner, the document used is the passport. The registration is not a formality you can skip or undo later without going back to the carrier.
Banking, delivery and platform accounts increasingly ask for a number registered to the account holder. A number registered to somebody else cannot be used as proof of identity, even if you are the one paying for it and carrying the phone.

## Before you go to the counter

Bring the passport you will keep using in China. The registration is tied to that document, so a passport you plan to replace soon creates extra work later.
Check that the passport is valid and that the name and number in it match what you will give the carrier. Decide which carrier you want and find a staffed store, not a reseller kiosk, because the identity check happens at the counter.
If someone offers to hand you a line already registered in their name, treat that as a problem, not a shortcut. It will not serve as proof of identity for banking or platform accounts.

## At the counter

The staff will ask for the passport and enter the details into the carrier's system. They may ask you to confirm your name and document number, and they will register the line to that identity before activating it.
You choose the line and the plan at the counter. Ask what the plan includes and what happens at the end of the period, and get the terms in writing or in the carrier's app rather than relying on what was said.
Keep the registration record and the receipt. You will need them if you later change the plan, replace the SIM, or are asked to show that the line is yours.

## After the line is active

Use the number in your own name when you open banking, delivery and platform accounts. Those services increasingly check that the number is registered to the account holder, and a line in your name passes that check.
If your passport changes, go back to the carrier and update the registration. The line stays linked to the identity document on file, so an outdated document causes friction when you need to prove the line is yours.
If you stop using the line, close it at the carrier rather than handing the SIM to someone else. A line registered to you remains registered to you until the carrier records a change.
