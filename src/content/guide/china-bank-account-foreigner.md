A Chinese bank account is opened at a branch counter, by you, with your passport in hand. The branch checks your identity and your right to be in the country, and it decides which class of account you get. Nothing here is legal, tax or immigration advice; the branch makes the final call on every point.

## What the branch is checking

The account is opened in person. There is no remote or online route for a foreign passport holder, so plan to be at the counter yourself.
The bank verifies identity against the passport and against the visa or residence permit inside it. Bring the physical passport, not a copy or a photo, and make sure the permit is valid on the day you go.
The name on the account must match the passport exactly, including the order of the names. Chinese passports and many others order family name first; if the clerk enters your names in the other order, the account will not match your documents later. Read the screen or the printout before you sign.

## Proof of address and residence registration

A proof of address is usually asked for. For a foreign resident, the residence registration is often what satisfies it, so deal with registration before you go to the bank.
If you are staying in a hotel, ask the front desk for the registration slip they file for foreign guests. If you are renting, the registration is normally handled with your landlord or the local office that covers your address. Bring the paper you were given, not a description of it.
Address documents differ by branch and by city. Ask the branch what it accepts before you queue, and ask again if you move.

## Account class decides what the account can do

Online banking and cross-border receipt differ between account classes, and the branch decides the class. A basic class may limit what you can do from the app or what can be sent to the account from abroad.
Say plainly what you need the account for: salary, paying rent, receiving money from family, or sending money out. The clerk can then tell you which class fits and what the limits are.
If the class you are given does not cover your needs, ask what would change it. Upgrading usually means returning to the branch with more documents, so it is worth settling at the first visit.

## After the account is open

Keep the passport and the permit valid. A permit that lapses can affect what the bank will let you do with the account, and renewing it may mean another visit to update the record.
Keep your Chinese phone number active. It is normally tied to the account for codes and alerts, and losing it can lock you out of the app.
Keep the registration paper and any address documents together with your banking paperwork, so the next visit does not start from nothing.
