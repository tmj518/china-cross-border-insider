## AI Summary

China's domestically built ocean drilling vessel, Mengxiang, completed a 33-day sea trial of the country's first self-developed 2,000-meter-class riserless mud recovery and circulation drilling system. The system reached a maximum operating water depth of 2,004.8 meters, surpassing the previous international record of 1,419 meters. This capability positions China to drill deeper for natural gas hydrates and minerals, which will shape future supply costs for importers of deep-sea resources and offshore oil.

## What happened

The Ministry of Natural Resources, reporting through China Daily, announced that the Mengxiang vessel completed a 33-day sea trial of China's first self-developed 2,000-meter-class riserless mud recovery and circulation drilling system. The trial took place in the northern South China Sea, where the system was deployed at two depths: 142.7 meters and 2,004.8 meters.

The 2,004.8-meter deployment exceeds the previous international depth record of 1,419 meters for similar systems. That record stood before this trial, and the new figure represents a 585.8-meter improvement over the prior benchmark.

The Mengxiang, commissioned in 2024, has now completed nine voyages including natural gas hydrate drilling. During this trial, the vessel conducted five drilling and coring tests, using four different mud densities to test wellhead liquid level balance. With this trial, all four drilling modes of the Mengxiang have completed functional verification.

> **Riserless mud recovery and circulation drilling system** — a drilling technology that recovers and recirculates drilling mud without a riser pipe connecting the seafloor to the surface vessel. This approach reduces the volume of drilling fluid discharged into the ocean and allows drilling operations in deeper water where conventional riser systems face technical limits.

## FAQ

**What is a riserless mud recovery and circulation drilling system?**

A riserless mud recovery and circulation drilling system recovers and recirculates drilling mud without a riser pipe connecting the seafloor to the surface vessel. In conventional drilling, a riser pipe carries drilling mud between the vessel and the wellhead. Riserless systems eliminate that pipe, which reduces the volume of drilling fluid discharged into the ocean and allows drilling in deeper water where riser systems face technical limits. The system tested on the Mengxiang recovers the mud, recirculates it, and maintains wellhead liquid level balance using different mud densities. This approach reduces environmental discharge and extends the depth range for drilling operations.
