A container does not move because the goods are ready. It moves because the
paperwork is complete. Most first-shipment delays trace back to one missing
document, discovered on the day the vessel was supposed to sail.

## The core document set

| Document | Issued by | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial invoice | Supplier | Declares value, description and terms of sale |
| Packing list | Supplier | Maps cartons, weights and dimensions to the shipment |
| Bill of lading / air waybill | Carrier or forwarder | Title document; without it the goods cannot be released |
| Certificate of origin | Chamber of commerce or authorised body | Determines preferential duty treatment |
| Export declaration | Supplier, filed with Chinese customs | Evidence the goods legally left China |
| Product certificates | Testing body, per destination market | Confirms the product may legally be sold where it lands |
| Insurance certificate | Insurer | Covers the leg your Incoterm says you own |

The first four are non-negotiable on every shipment. The rest depend on the
product and the destination.

## Incoterms decide the argument before it happens

Most disputes between a first-time importer and a supplier are not about
price. They are about which leg each party owns.

- **FOB (Free On Board):** the supplier delivers to the port of departure and
  clears export. You own the ocean freight, insurance and destination
  clearance. This is the default for buyers who want to control the freight
  forwarder.
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight):** the supplier arranges and pays freight
  and insurance to your port, but risk still transfers at the origin port.
  Convenient, and easy to overpay for if you never compare rates.
- **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):** the supplier handles everything to your door,
  including import duty. Simple on paper, and the option most likely to hide
  a duty estimate that turns out to be wrong.

Pick the term deliberately, write it into the purchase order, and make sure
the invoice repeats it exactly. A mismatch between purchase order and invoice
is a customs query waiting to happen.

## The mistakes that cost the most time

1. **Invoice description does not match the packing list.** Customs reads both.
2. **Value declared lower than the transaction.** The saving is small; the
   penalty and the hold are not.
3. **Certificate of origin requested after the vessel sails.** Some can be
   issued retrospectively; assuming they all can is how preferential duty is
   lost.
4. **No destination-market product certificate.** Goods can arrive legally and
   still be unsellable because they cannot clear the destination regulator.

## For you

- **First shipment:** ask the supplier for the invoice and packing list in
  draft, before production finishes. Errors are free to fix at that stage.
- **Repeat importer:** keep one template purchase order per product with the
  Incoterm, HS code and certificate list already filled in.
- **Anyone moving a new product category:** confirm the destination-market
  certificate requirement first. It is the only item on this list that cannot
  be solved with money and time after the fact.

## The point

Freight is quoted in days. Paperwork is where the weeks go. Build the document
set before you book the space — not after the goods are sitting at the port.
