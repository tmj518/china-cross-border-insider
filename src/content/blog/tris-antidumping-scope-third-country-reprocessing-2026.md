## AI Summary

Commerce preliminarily found Tris from China sold at less than fair value, with a period of investigation running from 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026 and provisional measures extended while the final determination is postponed. The part worth reading is the scope clause: Tris HCl made in a third country from Chinese Tris is covered, and recrystallising, retesting or repackaging in a third country does not remove the product from the investigation.

## What happened

The notice, published 25 November 2026 at 91 FR 76232, is a preliminary affirmative determination in an antidumping investigation of Tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane from China, case A-570-230. Commerce initiated the investigation on 18 May 2026 and postponed the preliminary determination once, on 4 September 2026, before issuing this one.

The operative sentence for anyone moving the chemical through a third country is in the scope section:

> "Tris HCl manufactured in a third country using Tris produced in China is subject to the investigation. In addition, reprocessing Tris or Tris HCl in a third country by, for example, recrystallizing, retesting, or repackaging the merchandise does not remove the product from the scope of this investigation."

The scope is governed by that written description. The notice lists HTSUS subheading 2922.19.9690 "for convenience and customs purposes" and states explicitly that the written description is dispositive.

## Why now

The determination arrives after a postponement, and it extends provisional measures rather than closing the case. That sequence matters to an importer because provisional measures are the part that generates cash exposure while the case runs.

The timing is also the substance: the period of investigation covers six months, from 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026. Entries inside that window are on the record; entries after it are covered by the provisional measures while the investigation continues.

## So what

Three things, and the second is the one that surprises people.

**The tariff code is not the test.** 2922.19.9690 is stated for convenience. A shipment that classifies correctly in that subheading is still outside the investigation if the written description does not reach it, and a shipment that tries to leave the subheading does not leave the scope.

**Reprocessing is not transformation.** Recrystallising, retesting and repackaging are named in the notice as things that do not remove the product from the scope. A third-country operation that consists of those steps is a step in the supply chain, not a change of origin.

**Tris HCl inherits Tris.** The notice says Tris HCl made in a third country from Chinese Tris is subject. That means the origin question moves one step upstream, from the finished salt to the Chinese Tris input.

## How confident

**What is settled.** The scope language, the six-month period of investigation, the initiation and postponement citations and the HTSUS subheading are all in the notice.

**What is not settled.** The final determination and the eventual rate. This is a preliminary result; the notice invites comment and extends provisional measures while the case continues.

**How to use this.** Check the input, not the output. If your supplier buys Chinese Tris and converts it, the conversion does not answer the question the notice asks.

## For you

**If you import specialty chemicals.** Ask for the origin of the Tris input, not the origin of the salt. The notice makes that distinction the test.

**If you are a trader or platform seller.** A document that says "made in [third country]" is not the document this investigation requires. What it requires is a statement about where the Tris was produced. The same discipline appears in [the steel circumvention rulings from the same week](/blog/steel-circumvention-vietnam-caught-indonesia-not-2026/), where the origin of the hot-rolled steel decided the outcome.

**If you are a broker or forwarder.** Scope disputes are decided on the written description before the entry is liquidated, not after. The classification advice in [the landed-cost note on CBAM](/blog/eu-cbam-cost-per-tonne-chinese-steel-aluminium-2026/) runs the same way: the number follows the document.

## FAQ

**Does moving production to a third country remove the duty?** Not if the operation is recrystallising, retesting or repackaging, and not if the Tris input is Chinese. The notice names those operations specifically.

**Is Tris HCl covered?** Yes, when it is manufactured in a third country using Tris produced in China. The notice states it is subject to the investigation.

**Does the HTSUS subheading decide coverage?** No. The notice lists subheading 2922.19.9690 for convenience and says the written description is dispositive.

**What is the period of investigation?** 1 October 2025 through 31 March 2026, a six-month window.
