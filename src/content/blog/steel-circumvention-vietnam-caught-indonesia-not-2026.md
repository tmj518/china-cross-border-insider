## AI Summary

On 24 November 2026 the US Commerce Department published two circumvention rulings on the same day: steel wheels completed in Vietnam from Chinese hot-rolled steel are circumventing the duties and carry a cash deposit from 19 March 2026, while corrosion-resistant steel completed in Indonesia from Chinese substrate is not. Same statute, same country-of-origin concern, opposite outcomes. For an importer the ruling is not a general statement about Southeast Asian routing; it is a finding about one product, one input and one record.

## What happened

Two notices, published one page apart in the same Federal Register issue, reach opposite conclusions on the same legal question.

The first, at 91 FR 75828, concerns certain steel wheels from China. Commerce initiated a country-wide circumvention inquiry on 19 March 2026 and selected NCC Vietnam as the mandatory respondent in August. Its determination:

> "Commerce preliminarily determines that steel wheels completed in Vietnam using Chinese-origin HRS and subsequently exported from Vietnam to the United States is circumventing the Orders on a country-wide basis."

The second, at 91 FR 75824, covers corrosion-resistant steel products (CORE). Commerce initiated that inquiry on 25 March 2026, sent quantity-and-value questionnaires to four Indonesian producers in April, and reached the opposite result: CORE completed in Indonesia using Chinese hot-rolled or cold-rolled substrate "is not circumventing the Orders on a country-wide basis".

Both notices are dated November 2026 and both take effect on 24 November 2026.

## Why now

The two cases were filed, initiated and decided within a week of each other because they test the same thing: whether processing in a third country changes the duty that applies to goods made from Chinese substrate.

For the wheels case, Commerce answers yes - the processing does not change the duty, and the merchandise is inside the scope of the orders. For CORE, it answers no. The difference is not geography. Vietnam is not disfavoured over Indonesia as a matter of policy; each ruling rested on its own record, its own respondents and its own analysis of the statutory criteria in section 781(b).

The wheels ruling goes further than a finding. Because the determination is country-wide and affirmative, Commerce directs Customs and Border Protection to suspend liquidation and require a cash deposit on unliquidated entries entered or withdrawn from warehouse on or after 19 March 2026 - the date the inquiry started, not the date of the ruling.

## So what

**The retroactive date is the expensive part.** A preliminary affirmative circumvention finding reaches back to the initiation date. An importer who shipped wheels through Vietnam in April, May or June has exposure that predates the November notice by eight months.

**The certification is the operational part.** The notice states that no Vietnamese company, including the mandatory respondent, is currently ineligible to certify that individual entries are not subject to cash deposits - but eligibility must be demonstrated in a certification review. A certification is a claim about the origin of the input, not about the assembly.

**The negative finding is not a safe harbour.** CORE from Indonesia is outside the orders on this record, and Commerce intends to verify the information before the final determination. A negative preliminary finding that reverses at verification is a live possibility; the notice says verification will happen.

## How confident

**What is settled.** The two outcomes, their Federal Register citations, the retroactive deposit date of 19 March 2026 and the CORE period of inquiry from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2025 are all stated in the notices.

**What is not settled.** The final determinations. Both are preliminary. The wheels notice sets a comment window and the ITC has 60 days from notification to give written advice on inclusion; the CORE notice defers its briefing schedule until after verification.

**How to use this.** Read the ruling as a statement about a product-and-input pair. The question for an importer is not "is Vietnam safe" but "is my product the product in the notice, and is my input Chinese-origin hot-rolled steel".

## For you

**If you import steel products from Southeast Asia.** Get the mill certificate for the substrate, not the assembly invoice. The wheel ruling turns on the origin of the hot-rolled steel, and no document about the finished wheel answers that. The same discipline applies when you [read a duty notice into a landed cost](/blog/section-301-exclusions-extended-january-2027/).

**If you are a platform seller sourcing through a trading company.** Ask which entity appears as the importer of record and which mill supplied the substrate. A trading company that cannot name the mill cannot tell you whether a certification is honest.

**If you are a forwarder or customs broker.** Two determinations on the same day in opposite directions is the case for reading the notice rather than the summary. The summary of the wheels ruling says "circumventing"; the summary of the CORE ruling says "not circumventing"; both are true and neither travels.

## The data point

Two notices, published 24 November 2026: 91 FR 75828 finds circumvention for steel wheels completed in Vietnam and requires cash deposits on entries from 19 March 2026; 91 FR 75824 finds no circumvention for CORE completed in Indonesia over a period of inquiry running five years, from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2025.

## FAQ

**Does the Vietnam ruling apply to every product finished in Vietnam?** No. It applies to certain steel wheels completed in Vietnam using Chinese-origin hot-rolled steel. The scope is defined by the orders on steel wheels from China, not by the country of finishing.

**Can a Vietnamese exporter certify its way out of the deposit?** The notice allows certification that individual entries are not subject to cash deposits, and says no company is currently ineligible - but eligibility must be demonstrated in a certification review, which can be verified.

**Why is the deposit retroactive?** Because the statute and the regulation tie suspension of liquidation to the initiation date of the inquiry. For the wheels case that date is 19 March 2026.

**Is CORE from Indonesia now exempt permanently?** No. The finding is preliminary, Commerce intends to verify, and the final determination follows. A negative preliminary result is a position on the current record.

**Where are the primary documents?** Federal Register 91 FR 75828 (steel wheels) and 91 FR 75824 (CORE), both published 24 November 2026.
