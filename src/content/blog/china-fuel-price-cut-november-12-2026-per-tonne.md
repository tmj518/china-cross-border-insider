## AI Summary

China's National Development and Reform Commission cut gasoline and diesel standard product prices by 645 and 625 yuan per tonne respectively, effective from 24:00 on November 12, 2026. The adjustment lowers fuel costs for inland trucking, rail, and last-mile delivery in China. Importers recalculate per-shipment transport budgets and renegotiate freight quotes that carry fuel surcharges. The cut is directional: it reduces operating cost, not the price of goods.

## What happened

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) published a price adjustment announcement on November 12, 2026, stating that from 24:00 on that date, domestic gasoline and diesel standard product prices would fall by 645 yuan per tonne and 625 yuan per tonne respectively. The announcement reads: "自11月12日24时起，国内汽、柴油（标准品）价格每吨分别下调645元、625元。" This translates to: from 24:00 on November 12, domestic gasoline and diesel (standard product) prices are reduced by 645 yuan and 625 yuan per tonne respectively.

The adjustment applies to the standard product grades that anchor China's retail fuel pricing formula. Provincial-level pricing bureaus then set the actual retail ceiling prices for each region based on the standard product adjustment, quality differentials, and local conditions. The NDRC announcement is the instrument that sets the national benchmark; the provincial notices that follow translate it into pump prices.

This is a periodic adjustment under China's fuel pricing mechanism, which links domestic retail prices to international crude oil price movements over a 10-working-day window. When the weighted average of international crude prices moves beyond a threshold, the NDRC adjusts domestic prices accordingly. The November 12 cut reflects a downward movement in the international crude basket during the preceding observation period.

> **Standard product** — the reference grade of gasoline or diesel on which China's national fuel price adjustment is calculated. Provincial authorities apply quality and regional differentials to the standard product price to set actual retail ceiling prices at the pump. The 645 and 625 yuan per tonne cuts apply to the standard product; the pump price change in a given province differs by the local differential.

## Why now

The NDRC adjusts domestic fuel prices every 10 working days under the established pricing mechanism. The November 12 adjustment reflects the movement of international crude oil prices during the preceding observation window. When the weighted average of the international crude basket falls, the domestic price follows downward after the mechanism's threshold is crossed.

The timing is mechanical, not discretionary. The announcement date of November 12 aligns with the 10-working-day cycle. The effective time of 24:00 on the same day means the new prices apply from the start of the next day's operations. Any fuel purchased before 24:00 on November 12 is priced at the old level; any fuel purchased after that timestamp is priced at the new level.

For importers, the relevant point is that the cut is already in effect. The adjustment is not a proposal or a draft; it is a published decision with a fixed effective timestamp. Transport contracts and freight quotes that reference fuel prices should be re-examined against the new level.

## So what

The cut reduces the cost of moving goods inside China. Diesel is the dominant fuel for trucking, which carries the majority of domestic freight. A 625 yuan per tonne cut in diesel translates directly into lower per-kilometre operating costs for every truck on the road. Gasoline matters more for light commercial vehicles and last-mile delivery vans, where the 645 yuan per tonne cut reduces urban distribution costs.

The arithmetic is straightforward. A truck that consumes roughly 30 litres of diesel per 100 kilometres, at a density of approximately 0.84 kilograms per litre, burns about 25.2 kilograms of diesel per 100 kilometres. A 625 yuan per tonne cut equals 0.625 yuan per kilogram. That is a saving of about 15.75 yuan per 100 kilometres, or roughly 0.16 yuan per kilometre. For a 1,000-kilometre haul, the saving is about 157.5 yuan per truck.

For a shipment moving by road from a coastal port to an inland warehouse, the fuel saving is a small but real reduction in total landed cost. Consider a 20-tonne container moving 1,200 kilometres inland. At the per-kilometre saving above, the fuel cost reduction is about 189 yuan per truck. Spread across 20 tonnes of cargo, that is about 9.45 yuan per tonne of goods. For a 40-foot container carrying 25 tonnes, the saving is about 7.56 yuan per tonne.

The cut also affects freight quotes. Many domestic trucking contracts include a fuel surcharge formula that tracks the published diesel price. When the NDRC cuts the diesel price, the fuel surcharge component of a freight quote should fall. Importers with standing transport contracts should check whether their agreement includes such a formula and whether the November 12 cut triggers a downward adjustment.

For importers negotiating new freight quotes, the cut is a bargaining point. A carrier quoting a rate built on the pre-cut diesel price is quoting high. The 625 yuan per tonne reduction in diesel should flow through to the per-shipment rate, at least in part. The exact pass-through depends on the carrier's cost structure, but the direction is clear.

The cut does not change the price of the goods themselves. It changes the cost of moving them. Importers who buy on an ex-works or FOB basis and arrange their own inland transport capture the saving directly. Importers who buy on a delivered-duty-paid basis see the saving only if the seller's transport contract is adjusted; the seller is not obligated to pass it through.

## FAQ

**What does the 645 and 625 yuan per tonne cut mean at the pump?**

The NDRC sets the standard product price, not the final pump price. Provincial pricing bureaus apply quality and regional differentials to the standard product to set the retail ceiling price in each province. The 645 yuan per tonne gasoline cut and the 625 yuan per tonne diesel cut are the benchmark adjustments. The actual pump price change in a given province differs by the local differential. For a typical diesel density of 0.84 kilograms per litre, a 625 yuan per tonne cut equals about 0.53 yuan per litre. For gasoline at a typical density of 0.72 kilograms per litre, a 645 yuan per tonne cut equals about 0.46 yuan per litre. These per-litre figures are derived from the announced per-tonne cuts and standard densities; the provincial notice for the specific region gives the exact pump price.

**When does the new fuel price take effect?**

The new prices take effect from 24:00 on November 12, 2026. Any fuel purchased before that timestamp is priced at the old level. Any fuel purchased after that timestamp is priced at the new level. The effective time is stated in the NDRC announcement. For transport operations, this means fuel bought on the morning of November 13 is already at the lower price. The next NDRC adjustment is scheduled for the following 10-working-day cycle, which determines how long this price level remains in effect before the next possible change.

**How does this fuel cut affect the cost of importing goods into China?**

The cut reduces the cost of inland transport, which is part of the landed cost for goods moving from a Chinese port to an inland destination. For a truck consuming about 25.2 kilograms of diesel per 100 kilometres, the 625 yuan per tonne cut saves about 15.75 yuan per 100 kilometres. Over a 1,200-kilometre haul, that is about 189 yuan per truck. Spread across a 20-tonne load, that is about 9.45 yuan per tonne of goods. The saving is small relative to the value of most cargo, but it is real and it applies to every shipment moving by road. Importers who arrange their own transport capture the saving directly; importers buying delivered-duty-paid need to ask the seller for the adjustment.
