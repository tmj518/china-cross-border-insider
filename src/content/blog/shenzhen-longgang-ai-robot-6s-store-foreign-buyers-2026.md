## What happened

Global Times reported on 16 November 2026 that Shenzhen's Longgang district hosts what it describes as the world's first robot 6S store and AI 6S store, together showcasing more than 200 devices. A staff member told the paper that around 30 percent of those devices are making their global debut or entering commercial application for the first time.

The same report puts the visitor mix during APEC meeting week at about 70 percent foreigners. On the Tuesday of that week, organisers planned an attempt at a Guinness World Record title with 150 robots performing a continuous coordinated movement for one hour.

The district context the report gives: Longgang is home to more than 13,000 AI and robotics companies, accounting for nearly one-fifth of Shenzhen's total. The avenue's official statement, as cited in the report, says the district has opened 793 application scenarios under its "City + AI" initiative and established a 1-billion-yuan ($150 million) AI industry master fund.

> **6S store** — a showroom format that extends the familiar sale, service and spare-parts model with additional functions the report does not itemise. Treat the label as a description of a physical venue where devices are displayed and demonstrated, not as a certification, a warranty or a distribution agreement.

What the report does not contain matters as much as what it does. There is no price for any device, no minimum order quantity, no export licence reference, no customs classification and no named manufacturer. A buyer reading it as a shopping list will arrive without the one thing a showroom cannot supply: a quotation.

## Why now

The timing is the APEC meeting week, which is why the visitor mix skewed so heavily foreign and why the display included debut devices rather than a settled catalogue. A concentration of first-time commercial entries in one venue is a narrow window: a device that is debuting this month is a device whose export paperwork, certification and after-sales arrangement are least likely to be settled.

That cuts both ways for a buyer. Early contact with a newly commercial product gets attention and negotiating room that a mature product line does not offer. It also means the supplier is learning its own export process at the same time the buyer is. The 13,000-company figure tells you the district has depth; it does not tell you that any given company on the avenue has shipped outside China before.

## So what

The decision this article answers is not "what does a robot cost." No figure in the report supports a cost calculation, and inventing a unit price to build a landed-cost table would produce a number with no document behind it. The decision is whether a trip to Longgang earns its cost, and that is answerable from the report's own structure.

Work it as a screening problem. The avenue shows more than 200 devices. If about 30 percent are debuting or newly commercial, that is the segment a buyer cannot see at a trade fair with a settled catalogue, and it is the segment where a first meeting is worth most. The remaining share is product a buyer can evaluate from a distance. So the trip's value concentrates on the debut share, and the trip should be built around it rather than around a general walk-through.

Second, the visitor mix. About 70 percent of visitors being foreign during APEC week means the venue is already set up to handle foreign buyers — language, samples, demonstrations. It does not mean the venue is set up to export. Those are different capabilities, and the gap between them is where a deposit goes wrong.

Third, the district numbers. More than 13,000 AI and robotics companies in one district, with 793 application scenarios opened under a city programme and a 1-billion-yuan master fund behind the sector. For a buyer, the fund and the scenarios are signals of local depth, not of export readiness. A company that wins domestic city scenarios has proven it can deliver to a Chinese public buyer. That is a useful reference and a different reference from a foreign importer's.

The arithmetic a buyer should run before booking is a comparison of two costs, and the report supplies neither. What it supplies is the shape: the number of candidate devices, the share that are new, and the share of the room that is foreign. A buyer who cannot put a number on the cost of the trip and the cost of a bad first order should not treat the showroom as a substitute for either.

For a buyer already sourcing robotics from China, the practical read is that Longgang is now a place where a shortlist can be built in a week rather than over months of email. For a buyer new to the category, the same venue is a place to learn what the current generation looks like before committing to a specification. Both are worth the trip; neither is worth a deposit on the day.

## For you

- **Importers planning a Q1 sourcing trip:** build the itinerary around the debut share, not the whole floor. Ask the venue in advance which devices are newly commercial and book meetings against that list before you fly.

## FAQ

**Is the Longgang robot 6S store open to foreign buyers outside APEC week?**

The report describes the venue during APEC meeting week, when about 70 percent of visitors were foreigners, and does not state an opening policy for other periods. Nothing in the material says access is restricted to the APEC window, and nothing says it is open year-round to foreign visitors. A buyer planning a trip should confirm access directly with the venue before booking travel, and should not assume the APEC-week visitor mix or the debut lineup carries over to another month.

**Does the report give any price for the robots on display?**

No. The Global Times report gives a device count, a debut share, a visitor mix, a district company count, a scenario count, a fund size and a record-attempt robot count. It contains no unit price, no FOB quotation, no minimum order quantity and no payment terms. A buyer cannot build a landed-cost model from this material, and any per-unit figure presented as coming from it would be invented. The report is a signal that the venue exists and draws foreign buyers, not a price list.

**What does the 1-billion-yuan AI industry master fund mean for a foreign importer?**

The report attributes the fund to the avenue's official statement and gives its size only. It does not describe the fund's terms, its eligibility rules, its investment stage or any application route, and it does not connect the fund to foreign buyers or to export activity. Read it as evidence that the district is putting public money behind the sector, which supports the depth implied by the 13,000-company count. It is not a subsidy a foreign importer can apply for on the basis of this report.
