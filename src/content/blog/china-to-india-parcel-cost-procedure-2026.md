## Definition

The object this page describes is the import procedure for a parcel moving from China to India - the sequence of declaration, documentation and clearance steps that a consignment passes through, and the conditions at each step that decide whether it proceeds, is held, or is sent back.

## What happened

The search that produced this page is a cost search. Someone has goods in China, a buyer in India, and wants to know what the parcel charges will be. The honest answer is that the freight quote is the last number in the chain, not the first, and the steps before it are the ones that decide whether the quote is the whole cost or a fraction of it.

A parcel from China to India is not one transaction. It is a Chinese export, an international movement, and an Indian import, and each of the three has its own declaration and its own document set. The freight charge covers the middle one. The two ends are where a parcel is stopped, held, or assessed on a value the shipper did not expect.

That is why this page is written as a procedure rather than as a price list. A shipper who knows the order of the steps can ask the forwarder the right question at each one. A shipper who only has a freight quote has a number that covers the part of the journey where nothing usually goes wrong.

## Why now

Three things make the procedure worth reading before the parcel is booked rather than after it lands.

The first is that the declaration route is chosen at the point of shipping and is not repairable afterwards. A parcel declared one way is treated that way on arrival, and assembling documents after the fact does not change the declaration that was made. This is the same structural point that governs the return direction, and [the procedure behind a return to China](/blog/china-return-shipping-refund-procedure-2026/) sets out the mirror image of it: the route decision belongs before the goods move.

The second is that the document set at the Chinese end has been moving. The origin and inspection paperwork that a consignment leaves China with is not the same set it was a year ago, and [the documents that decide whether a container moves](/blog/importing-from-china-documents-checklist/) covers which of them are non-negotiable on every consignment and which depend on the product and the destination.

The third is that a parcel and a container are cleared by different machinery even when they carry the same goods. The procedure below is written for the parcel, because that is the question that was asked, and the differences from the container route are exactly where a small shipper gets caught.

## So what

The procedure runs in a fixed order, and each step has a condition that stops it.

| Step | What happens | What it needs | What stops it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classification | The goods are assigned a heading that decides how they are treated on both ends | A description specific enough to classify | A vague description, which is the most common reason a parcel is held at the first step |
| Export declaration in China | The consignment is declared out | The commercial invoice and the packing list, matching each other | Invoice and packing list disagreeing on quantity, value or description |
| Movement | The parcel travels by the booked route | The air waybill or bill of lading | A waybill that does not name the same parties as the invoice |
| Import declaration in India | The consignment is declared in | The declaration referencing the same goods, quantities and parties | A declaration that does not reconcile with the export documents |
| Assessment | The consignment is assessed on its declared value and classification | The invoice, and whatever the assessing office asks for to support it | A declared value the assessing office does not accept |
| Release or hold | The parcel is released, or held pending further documents | The documents requested at the hold | A document that cannot be produced, which is where a parcel is returned or abandoned |

Read the table from the top. Each row's failure mode is the next row's missing input, which is why a parcel that fails at classification cannot be rescued at assessment.

**The order matters more than any single step.** A shipper who books freight first and classifies second has bought a movement for goods whose treatment is still undecided. A shipper who classifies first, then declares, then books, has a quote that covers a journey the goods are actually allowed to make.

**The documents are the same documents at both ends.** The invoice, the packing list and the transport document are the spine of the file, and the test applied to them is reconciliation: the same goods, the same quantities, the same parties, on every page. A parcel is not usually stopped because a document is missing. It is stopped because two documents disagree.

**The value question is the one that moves the cost.** The freight charge is quoted. The assessment is not. A declared value that the assessing office does not accept turns into a query, and a query turns into a hold, and a hold is where the cost of a parcel stops being the freight quote and starts being the time the goods sit still.

## For you

- **Shippers who have a freight quote and nothing else:** ask the forwarder which declaration route the parcel will travel under, and get it in writing before the parcel is collected. The route is the decision that cannot be revisited.
- **Shippers sending samples or small orders:** classify the goods before booking. A description specific enough to classify is the cheapest document in the file and the one most often written last.
- **Shippers whose supplier prepares the invoice:** check the invoice against the packing list line by line. A disagreement between the two is the most common reason a parcel stops at the first step.
- **Shippers who have been quoted a single all-in figure:** ask what happens to that figure if the assessing office queries the declared value. An all-in quote that assumes a clean assessment is a quote for the easy case.

## FAQ

**Why does this page not give me a shipping cost from China to India?**
Because no official page fetched in this run publishes one, and a number without a source is worse than no number. The three indexes attempted are listed under sources and none returned a page. Freight rates are quoted commercially by carriers and forwarders rather than published as a single official rate, and the duty and assessment side depends on the classification and the declared value of the specific goods. A page that gave a single figure for shipping from China to India would be describing a parcel it had never seen.

**What can I do with the procedure if I do not have the numbers?**
Use it to ask better questions. The procedure tells you that the declaration route is decided before the parcel moves and cannot be changed after, that the invoice, packing list and transport document must reconcile with each other, and that the assessment step depends on a declared value the assessing office accepts. Each of those is a question you can put to a forwarder or a supplier today, and each of them changes what the parcel costs when it goes wrong.

**Which document is most likely to stop my parcel?**
The one that disagrees with another one. A parcel is rarely stopped because a document is absent; it is stopped because the invoice and the packing list state different quantities, or because the waybill names a different party from the invoice. Reconciliation across the file is the test, and it is a test the shipper can run before the parcel is collected at no cost.

**Does the same procedure apply to a full container?**
The order is the same and the machinery is not. A container and a parcel are cleared through different routes even when they carry the same goods, and the document set at the Chinese end has been changing. The documents checklist linked above covers the container side, including which documents are non-negotiable on every consignment and which depend on the product and the destination.

**What should I do if the assessing office queries my declared value?**
Produce the documents that support the value rather than restating it. The query is about whether the declared value is supported, so the answer is the invoice, the contract or order, and the payment record, reconciled with each other. A restated value without supporting documents is the reason a query becomes a hold.
