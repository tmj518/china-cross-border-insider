The excise tax is calculated on the CIF value of the vehicle, which includes the cost of the goods, insurance, and freight. It is an ad valorem tax, meaning it is a percentage of the value rather than a fixed amount per unit. > **Excise tax** — a tax levied on specific goods at the point of import or sale, calculated as a percentage of the CIF value for imported vehicles. Unlike a customs duty, which is collected by customs authorities, an excise tax is collected by the excise department and is typically included in the final retail price.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between excise tax and customs duty?**

Excise tax is a tax on specific goods, calculated as a percentage of the CIF value, and is collected by the excise department. Customs duty is a tax on imported goods, also calculated on the CIF value, but collected by customs authorities. Both are added to the landed cost of a vehicle. For an imported EV, the importer pays both the customs duty and the excise tax, plus any other applicable fees such as VAT. The excise tax is typically included in the retail price, while the customs duty is a cost borne by the importer before the vehicle reaches the showroom.

**Does the 35% rate apply to all electric vehicles, or only certain types?**

The directive specifies that the 35% rate applies to imported BEVs (battery electric vehicles) and REEVs (range-extended electric vehicles). It does not mention hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) or plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) that do not have a range extender. Importers should confirm with Thai customs or the excise department whether their specific vehicle model falls under the BEV or REEV classification. The classification determines which rate applies, and a misclassification can result in underpayment or overpayment of excise tax.

**Can I avoid the new rate by shipping before 1 January 2027?**

The effective date is 1 January 2027. If your customs declaration is accepted before that date, the current rate applies. If the declaration is accepted on or after 1 January 2027, the 35% rate applies, regardless of when the vehicle was loaded or when the vessel departed. This means importers with cargo already on the water should prioritize fast-track clearance to ensure the declaration is filed before the deadline. However, the directive does not specify whether in-transit cargo is exempt, so importers should confirm with their customs broker.
