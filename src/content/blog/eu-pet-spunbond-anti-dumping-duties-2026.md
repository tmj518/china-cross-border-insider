## What happened

The European Commission published its definitive anti-dumping measure on PET Spunbond from China on 30 October 2026. The definitive duties range from **39.8% to 48.1%** and remain in force for **five years** from that date.

These definitive rates replace the provisional duties that had applied since June 2026. The provisional range was **45.6% to 50%**. The definitive measure therefore sits below the provisional range at both ends: the low end drops from 45.6% to 39.8%, and the high end drops from 50% to 48.1%.

The product scope covers PET Spunbond, a nonwoven fabric used in hygiene products, medical textiles, filtration and construction. The EU market for this product is worth **€200m**, which is the value the Commission used to assess the significance of the imports during the investigation.

The measure is an anti-dumping duty, not a safeguard. It targets imports originating in China specifically, and the rate an individual importer pays depends on which exporter produced the goods. The Commission assigns each cooperating exporter an individual duty rate within the 39.8% to 48.1% range; all other exporters face the highest rate in the range.

> **Anti-dumping duty** — a customs duty imposed on imports from a specific country when the exporting country sells the product below its normal value and that dumping causes injury to the importing country's domestic industry. Unlike a safeguard, which applies to all origins, an anti-dumping duty is country-specific and exporter-specific.

## FAQ

**Which duty rate applies to my shipment?**

The rate depends on which Chinese exporter produced the goods. The European Commission assigns each cooperating exporter an individual duty rate within the 39.8% to 48.1% range. If your supplier cooperated with the investigation, they will know their rate. If your supplier did not cooperate, or you cannot confirm their status, the residual rate of 48.1% applies. Ask your supplier for their cooperating status and the duty rate assigned to them in the definitive regulation. The rate is fixed for the five-year duration of the measure, so you only need to confirm it once.

**Is the duty calculated on the FOB or CIF value?**

The anti-dumping duty is calculated on the CIF value of the goods — the cost of the goods plus insurance and freight to the EU border. This is the standard basis for EU anti-dumping duties. The CIF value is higher than the FOB value because it includes the freight and insurance costs, so the duty amount is higher than it would be on an FOB basis. Your customs broker will use the CIF value declared on the customs entry to calculate the duty. Make sure your commercial invoice and freight documents support the declared CIF value.

**Does the definitive duty replace the provisional duty retroactively?**

No. The definitive duty applies from 30 October 2026 forward. Shipments that cleared under the provisional duties between June 2026 and 30 October 2026 paid the provisional rate, and that payment is final. The definitive duty replaces the provisional duty for shipments cleared on or after 30 October 2026. The definitive rates are lower than the provisional rates at both ends of the range, but importers who paid the provisional rate will not receive a refund for the difference. The five-year clock starts on 30 October 2026, so the measure runs until 30 October 2031 unless a review changes it.
