## AI Summary

China's National Development and Reform Commission cut the wholesale ceiling on standard-grade diesel by 625 yuan a tonne and on standard-grade gasoline by 645 yuan a tonne, effective 24:00 on November 12, 2026. The cut lowers the fuel line inside every inland trucking quote and every factory-gate fuel surcharge that is indexed to the state ceiling. Importers holding quotes dated before the change should ask carriers and suppliers to restate them against the new per-tonne figure.

## What happened

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) published a price adjustment notice for domestic refined oil products. From 24:00 on November 12, 2026, the domestic price of standard-grade gasoline and standard-grade diesel is cut by 645 yuan and 625 yuan per tonne respectively.

The notice states the trigger plainly: the average price over the ten working days before this adjustment was lower than the average price over the ten working days before the previous adjustment. That is the mechanism. The per-tonne figure is the ceiling the state sets; retail and wholesale prices below it are set by the market.

The notice also restates the reporting channel. Consumers can report price violations through the 12315 platform. That is the enforcement line attached to the ceiling, not a separate measure.

> **Per-tonne ceiling** — the maximum wholesale price the state sets for a standard grade of refined fuel, expressed in yuan per tonne rather than yuan per litre. A cut to the ceiling lowers the input cost of every downstream user of that fuel, including trucking fleets and factories that run their own generators, without the state setting the price each of them actually pays.

The two grades move by different amounts, and the difference matters for who benefits:

| Grade | Cut (yuan per tonne) | Effective from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard-grade diesel | 625 | 24:00 on November 12, 2026 |
| Standard-grade gasoline | 645 | 24:00 on November 12, 2026 |

Diesel is the grade that moves cargo. Gasoline is the grade that moves people and light vehicles. An importer's inland trucking cost sits on the diesel line, so the 625 figure is the one to carry into a freight quote.

## Why now

The notice gives its own reason and no other: the ten-working-day average price before this adjustment came in below the ten-working-day average before the previous one. That is the whole of the stated timing. The adjustment is a scheduled repricing against a moving average, not a response to a single event.

The same week carried a separate monetary signal. The People's Bank of China has run overnight operations each working day since November 2, 2026 at a policy rate of 1.25 percent, as reported by China Daily. That is a different instrument with a different purpose, and it does not set the fuel ceiling. It is worth knowing only because a cheaper overnight rate and a cheaper diesel ceiling both ease the cash cost of moving goods in the same quarter.

## So what

The judgement is short. A cut to the per-tonne ceiling is a cut to the fuel input inside every inland leg, and the importer who does not restate the quote keeps paying the old number. The arithmetic is where the money is.

Start with the unit. The ceiling is quoted per tonne. A trucking quote is usually built per kilometre or per container move, with a fuel component inside it. The bridge between the two is the fleet's own consumption, which the importer does not control and the notice does not publish. So the honest way to use the 625 figure is as a ceiling on the fuel line, not as a per-kilometre rate.

Work it as a ratio. Take the fuel component a carrier has written into a quote and express it as a share of the total. The ceiling fell by 625 yuan per tonne of diesel. If the carrier's fuel cost per tonne of diesel consumed is the ceiling itself, the fuel line falls by 625 yuan for every tonne of diesel the move burns. Multiply the tonnes burned by 625 and you have the maximum reduction the ceiling supports on that leg. Anything the carrier concedes above that is a commercial decision, not a pass-through.

Run the same logic on a factory-gate surcharge. Many Chinese factories quote an ex-works price with a fuel surcharge indexed to the state ceiling. When the ceiling moves, the surcharge should move with it. The importer's job is to ask for the index, confirm it is the NDRC per-tonne figure, and check that the surcharge was restated on the November 12 basis rather than left on the prior one.

Three worked cases show where the money lands.

**Case one, a full-container inland leg.** A 40-foot container moving from a factory in the interior to a coastal port burns diesel on the road leg. The carrier's quote carries a fuel line. The ceiling fell by 625 yuan per tonne of diesel. If the carrier discloses the tonnes burned for the move, multiply by 625 and that is the ceiling on the reduction. If the carrier does not disclose it, ask for the fuel line as a share of the total and hold the carrier to the ratio.

**Case two, a factory-gate surcharge on a bulk order.** A factory sells ex-works with a fuel surcharge per tonne of product. The surcharge is indexed to the diesel ceiling. The ceiling fell by 625 yuan per tonne of diesel. The factory's surcharge should fall by the same 625 yuan for each tonne of diesel its production and internal handling burns per tonne of product. Ask the factory for that consumption ratio. Without it, the surcharge is a number the factory chose, not a pass-through.

**Case three, a multi-leg route.** Cargo moves factory to consolidation point, consolidation point to port, port to vessel. Each leg has its own carrier and its own fuel line. The 625 yuan per tonne cut applies to the diesel each leg burns. Sum the tonnes across the legs, multiply by 625, and that is the ceiling on the total reduction across the route. A carrier that restates one leg and not the others has captured the cut rather than passed it on.

The gasoline figure of 645 yuan per tonne is the one to use for light-vehicle legs and for any staff or sample movement priced on gasoline. It is a larger cut per tonne than diesel, but it touches a smaller share of an importer's landed cost.

The 12315 line is the backstop. If a supplier or carrier refuses to restate a surcharge that is contractually indexed to the state ceiling, the reporting channel is open. That is a contractual dispute first and a price-violation report second, and the importer should read the index clause before reaching for the hotline.

And a subsequent NDRC notice reversing the cut would reset the baseline the importer is repricing against.

## FAQ

**Does the cut apply to fuel already in a truck's tank or a factory's storage on November 12?**

The notice sets the domestic price from 24:00 on November 12, 2026. Fuel bought before that timestamp was bought at the prior ceiling. Fuel bought after it is bought at the new one. The importer's exposure is on the fuel burned after the change, which is why the restatement date on a carrier or factory quote matters more than the date the cargo moved. Ask for the restatement date in writing and hold the counterparty to it.

**Is the 625 yuan per tonne figure the amount my trucking cost falls?**

No. The figure is the cut to the wholesale ceiling on a tonne of standard-grade diesel. A trucking quote is built per kilometre or per container move, with a fuel component inside it. The ceiling caps the fuel line, not the freight rate. To get the ceiling on the reduction for a leg, multiply the tonnes of diesel that leg burns by 625. Without the consumption figure, the importer has a ceiling and a ratio, not a per-kilometre saving.

**What do I do if my supplier says the surcharge is not indexed to the state ceiling?**

Read the contract. If the surcharge is indexed to the NDRC per-tonne figure, the supplier should restate it on the November 12 basis. If the contract names a different index, such as a retail pump price or a provincial figure, the national ceiling does not bind the surcharge and the importer negotiates on the contract's own terms. Where a supplier refuses to honour an index it agreed to, the 12315 platform is the reporting channel named in the notice.
