A 15.7% year-on-year jump in industrial profits sounds like macro noise. For
anyone negotiating factory pricing, it is a clock.

## What happened

The National Bureau of Statistics reported August industrial profits up 15.7%
year on year, with equipment manufacturing leading at 21.3%. Margins improved
while order volumes grew more slowly — meaning factories are earning more per
unit shipped, not shipping dramatically more units.

Container throughput at the major export ports rose 6.2%, roughly a third of
the profit growth rate.

## Why now

Three years of margin compression forced capacity out of the system. Weak
producers closed; the survivors consolidated order books and now price with
discipline. August is the first month where that discipline shows up as
profit rather than as idle capacity.

## So what

Most buyers assume a recovering factory is a hungry factory. The opposite is
true. A profitable factory quotes higher, holds terms longer, and stops
absorbing tooling costs. If you need Q1 2027 capacity at 2026 pricing, the
quote you receive in November is the quote you keep.

I have run this play twice. In 2021 I waited until January to lock a
kitchen-gadget program and paid 9% more than the October quote I had been
handed. The second time, in 2024, I signed in November and held the price
through the spring.

## For you

- **New sellers:** get three quotes in October. The spread between them is
  your negotiating room.
- **Brand sellers:** lock annual volume commitments now, with a price-adjust
  clause tied to steel and resin indices rather than a flat number.
- **Arbitrage sellers:** shift to categories where factories are still running
  under capacity — those are the last places with slack pricing.
