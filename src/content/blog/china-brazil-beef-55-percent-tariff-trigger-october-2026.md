## What happened

The Ministry of Commerce of China (MOFCOM) issued notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号 on 30 September 2026, confirming that imports of beef from Brazil under the safeguard measure had reached 100% of the country-specific quota set by MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025. The quota was filled on 29 September 2026.

The notice states that from the third day after the quota reached 100% — that is, from 00:00 on 1 October 2026 — beef imported from Brazil will be subject to a 55% additional tariff on top of the current applicable duty rate. The additional tariff applies to the full value of the goods, not to the portion of the quota that was exceeded.

The mechanism is automatic. The notice does not describe any administrative discretion to delay the trigger, any exemption for in-transit cargo, or any grandfathering for contracts signed before the quota filled. The effective date is fixed at 1 October 2026 00:00.

> **Additional tariff** — a duty levied on top of the current applicable tariff rate for a product. For Brazilian beef, the 55% additional tariff is calculated on the same base as the existing duty, so the total duty payable rises by 55 percentage points of the customs value, not by 55% of the existing duty amount.

The following table shows the additional cost for three common shipment sizes, using a customs value of USD 4,500 per tonne for frozen beef:

| Shipment size (tonnes) | Customs value (USD) | 55% additional tariff (USD) | Additional cost per tonne (USD) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 90,000 | 49,500 | 2,475 |
| 27 (full 40ft reefer container) | 121,500 | 66,825 | 2,475 |
| 100 | 450,000 | 247,500 | 2,475 |

The per-tonne figure is the same across all shipment sizes because the additional tariff is ad valorem — a percentage of value, not a fixed amount per unit. What changes with shipment size is the total cash exposure.

## Why now

The quota was set in MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025, which established the annual country-specific volume for Brazil under the beef safeguard measure. Import volumes from Brazil accelerated through September 2026 and reached the full 100% threshold on 29 September 2026.

The notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号, published the following day, confirmed the trigger and the effective date. The third-day rule is written into the notice: the additional tariff starts on the third day after the quota reaches 100%, which lands on 1 October 2026 at 00:00.

The timing matters because the notice was published after the quota had already filled. Importers with cargo in transit on 29 September had no advance warning of the exact exhaustion date. The 3-day window between quota fill and tariff activation is the only buffer the measure provides.

## So what

The 55% additional tariff is a cost increase, not a marginal one. It applies to the full customs value of every Brazilian beef shipment cleared on or after 1 October 2026, regardless of when the goods were loaded, when the contract was signed, or when the vessel departed Brazil.

For a 27-tonne reefer container with a customs value of USD 121,500, the additional tariff adds USD 66,825. That is more than the typical ocean freight and insurance cost for the same container. For a 100-tonne shipment, the additional tariff reaches USD 247,500 — a sum that can exceed the entire profit margin on the consignment.

The critical timestamp is the customs declaration acceptance time, not the vessel arrival time. A shipment that arrives at port on 30 September but clears on 2 October pays the full 55% additional tariff. A shipment that clears on 30 September and sits in a bonded warehouse until November does not.

Importers with cargo already at port should prioritise clearance before 1 October 2026, provided all documentation is complete and valid. Importers with cargo on the water face a different decision: absorb the additional tariff, divert the shipment to another market, or renegotiate the contract with the Brazilian supplier. The arithmetic for each option starts with the same number — 55% of the customs value.

For importers who decide to continue buying Brazilian beef, the additional tariff is now a permanent part of the landed cost calculation until the safeguard measure expires or the quota is reset. The same arithmetic applies to every future shipment, not just the ones currently in transit.

## FAQ

**Does the 55% additional tariff apply to beef that was loaded before 1 October 2026?**

Yes, if the customs declaration is accepted on or after 1 October 2026. The notice sets the effective date at 00:00 on 1 October 2026 and describes no exemption for goods loaded, shipped, or contracted before that date. The trigger is the declaration acceptance time, not the loading date, the vessel departure date, or the contract signing date. A shipment that left Brazil in September but clears Chinese customs in October pays the full 55% additional tariff. A shipment that clears in September does not, even if it is still in port storage.

**Is the 55% additional tariff calculated on the existing duty or on the customs value?**

On the customs value. The notice states that the additional tariff is levied on top of the current applicable duty rate, which means the 55% is added to the existing duty percentage, not multiplied by it. If the current applicable duty rate is 12%, the total duty rate becomes 67% of the customs value. The additional tariff itself is 55% of the customs value, regardless of what the existing duty rate is. This is why the per-tonne cost increase is the same across all shipment sizes.

**What happens if the quota resets for 2027?**

The notice does not address the 2027 quota. MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025 set the country-specific volume for Brazil for the 2026 safeguard period. A new announcement would be required to set the 2027 quota. If a new quota is set and Brazilian imports stay below it, the additional tariff would not apply to shipments within the new quota. If the quota is filled again, the same third-day trigger would activate the 55% additional tariff. No such announcement has been published as of 8 November 2026.
