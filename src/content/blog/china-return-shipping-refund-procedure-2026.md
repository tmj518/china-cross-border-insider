## Definition

The procedure this article describes is the Chinese export tax refund on goods that were exported and are then returned — the route by which the exporter who originally declared the export recovers the value-added tax treatment on goods that come back, and it applies to the exporter of record on the original export declaration, not to the overseas buyer who is sending the parcel back.

> **Export tax refund on returned goods** — the adjustment made in the Chinese exporter's tax account when goods that were declared out are declared back in, claimed by the exporter of record on the original export declaration and not by the overseas buyer who sends the parcel back.

## What happened

The question behind this page is a seller's question: goods are coming back to China, and the seller wants to know what the return costs and what comes back. The answer splits in two, and only one half is the seller's.

The refund half belongs to the supplier. The export tax refund is paid to the entity that declared the goods out of China. When the goods return, the adjustment is made in that entity's tax account. A platform seller shipping a return to a supplier is not the applicant and does not receive the refund. Whether any of it reaches the seller is a commercial question between the two of them, not a customs one.

The cost half belongs to whoever agreed to pay the freight. That is a contract term, and it is decided long before the parcel moves.

What makes this page worth writing is that the two halves are routinely confused. Sellers ask what the refund is worth as though it were a discount on the return, and suppliers answer as though the return were free because a refund exists. Neither is true. The refund is the supplier's, the freight is whoever signed for it, and the only thing that decides whether the refund route opens at all is how the return was declared.

## Why now

Three things make the procedure worth understanding before the next return rather than after it.

The first is that the customs route is chosen at the point of shipping and cannot be repaired afterwards. Goods that re-enter under an ordinary import declaration are treated as an import. The refund route does not open later, and no amount of documentation assembled after arrival changes that.

The second is that the export side of the same mechanism has been moving. The rebate the supplier receives on the way out has been cut and cancelled for whole product categories, which changes what a supplier is willing to absorb on a return. [The 2026 export rebate removal for battery and solar products](/blog/china-export-tax-rebate-removal-battery-solar/) prices that change from the export direction; this page covers the return direction of the same account.

The third is that a return is usually a defect claim, and a defect claim is a negotiation. The seller who knows which party declares the return and on what basis is negotiating with the right question. The seller who asks what the rebate is worth is negotiating with the wrong one.

## So what

The practical consequence is that a seller should stop treating the refund as a recoverable cost and start treating the declaration route as the thing under their control.

| Question | Who answers it | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who declared the goods out of China | The supplier, from the original export declaration | Who can claim the refund at all |
| How the return is declared | Whoever books the return shipment | Whether the refund route opens |

## For you

- **Platform sellers shipping a defect return to a Chinese supplier:** decide the declaration route with the supplier before the parcel moves, and put in writing who declares the return and on what basis.
- **Sellers negotiating a replacement rather than a refund:** ask what the supplier's refund position does to the price of the replacement, because that is the number that moves, not the freight.
- **Sellers whose supplier is a trading company rather than the factory:** confirm which entity appears on the original export declaration, because that entity is the only one that can apply.
- **Sellers using a forwarder for the return leg:** give the forwarder the original export declaration details, so the return paperwork names the same goods, quantities and parties.

## FAQ

**Is the refund on a return to China paid to me as the seller?**
No. The export tax refund is paid to the entity that declared the goods out of China, and the adjustment on a return is made in that entity's tax account. A platform seller shipping goods back to a supplier is not the applicant. Whether any part of the refund reaches the seller depends on the commercial agreement between the two of them, not on customs or tax rules. The useful thing to establish in writing is who declares the return and on what basis, because that determines whether the refund route opens at all.

**Can I still claim the refund if the goods already re-entered as an ordinary import?**
No. The customs route is chosen at the point of shipping, and goods that re-enter under an ordinary import declaration are treated as an import. The refund route does not open afterwards, and assembling documentation after arrival does not change the declaration that was made. This is why the route decision belongs before the parcel moves, and why it is worth confirming with the supplier rather than leaving it to a forwarder booking the return leg on the cheapest basis available.

**Which documents have to exist before anything is filed?**
The original export customs declaration for the shipment; the import declaration under which the returned goods re-entered China, carrying the reference back to the original export; the commercial documents for the return, meaning the invoice and the contract or return agreement; the freight and insurance documents for the return leg; and the exporter's own tax registration and refund filing credentials, current for the period of the application. A missing freight document or a missing contract stops the application at intake, before any substantive review begins.

**Why do refund applications on returned goods get sent back?**
Mostly for documentary reasons rather than substantive ones. The return was not declared as a return; the declarations do not reconcile on quantities, product descriptions or parties; the file is incomplete; the application is filed outside the period that applies to the exporter; or the wrong entity applies, such as a related company, a forwarder or the overseas buyer filing in place of the exporter of record. Each of these costs a filing cycle, and the first cannot be fixed at all once the goods are in.
