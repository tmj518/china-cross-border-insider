## AI Summary

China's Beidou network now operates nearly 42 million industrial terminal devices, up about 27 percent year on year in the first half of 2026. More than 38 million of those units sit across 14 key industrial sectors. For importers, this means Beidou-based tracking on Chinese freight and vehicles is now a dependable default rather than a niche option. The cost consequence is directional: expect tracking capability, not a price change.

## What happened

The Global Navigation Satellite System and Location-Based Services Association of China published its Beidou industry development report, cited by China Daily on 18 November 2026. The report states that as of the first half of 2026, the number of Beidou terminal devices in operation across China — excluding consumer electronics — stood at nearly 42 million units. That represents an increase of nearly 9 million units from the same period last year, or year-on-year growth of about 27 percent.

The report also breaks down deployment across sectors. More than 38 million Beidou terminal units were deployed across 14 key industrial sectors, posting year-on-year growth of more than 26 percent. In the consumer market, about 124 million smartphones were produced and placed on the domestic market, more than 98 percent of which support Beidou services. Approximately 10 million passenger vehicles equipped with Beidou terminals were put on the Chinese market, and sales of electric bicycles with Beidou positioning capabilities totaled 26 million units.

The overall value of satellite-enabled navigation and positioning services in China was about 629 billion yuan ($94.37 billion) by the end of 2025, representing a 9.24 percent increase year-on-year.

> **Beidou terminal device** — a hardware unit that receives signals from China's BeiDou Navigation Satellite System to determine position, velocity and time. In logistics, these devices are embedded in trucks, containers, vessels, pallets and cargo locks to transmit location data over cellular or satellite networks.

## Why now

The report covers the first half of 2026, and the numbers were released in November 2026. The timing matters for two reasons. First, the year-on-year growth of about 27 percent in industrial terminals shows the network is expanding at a pace that makes Beidou-based tracking a standard feature of Chinese logistics, not a pilot project. Second, the report's publication gives importers a single, citable document to judge the state of the network.

The growth is concentrated in the industrial sectors that move freight: transportation, logistics, construction and energy. When more than 38 million terminals sit across those sectors, the infrastructure for tracking cargo is already in place. The report does not state which of the 14 sectors grew fastest, and it does not name a policy or investment that drove the growth. The numbers describe the state of the network, not the reason for it.

## So what

For an importer, the question is not whether Beidou works. The question is whether you can rely on it as the default tracking layer for cargo moving through China. The numbers say yes.

Nearly 42 million industrial terminals means the hardware is embedded in the vehicles, containers and equipment that move freight. When a trucking company in China quotes you a rate that includes live tracking, the tracking almost certainly runs on Beidou. When a port operator gives you a container position update, the update comes from a Beidou terminal. The network is no longer something you request as a special feature; it is the baseline.

The cost consequence is directional rather than priced. The report does not state what a Beidou terminal costs, what a tracking service subscription costs, or how the network changes freight rates. What the report does establish is scale. Scale drives down unit costs, and unit costs drive down the price of tracking. An importer who paid a premium for GPS-based tracking five years ago now pays a standard rate for Beidou tracking, or gets it bundled into the freight quote.

The practical arithmetic for an importer is about coverage, not price. If you ship 100 containers a year out of Chinese ports, and each container carries a Beidou terminal, you have position data on every shipment. If you ship through a freight forwarder whose fleet runs Beidou terminals, you have the same visibility. The report's numbers tell you the coverage is there. The report does not tell you which forwarders use it, which ports transmit the data, or how often the data updates.

For cargo in transit, the relevant comparison is with the alternatives. GPS remains available, but Beidou is the domestic default in China. A forwarder that offers GPS tracking on Chinese domestic legs is running a second system alongside the Beidou network. That adds cost and complexity for no additional coverage. The report's numbers suggest the efficient choice is to accept Beidou tracking as the standard and ask for the data feed, not for a different satellite system.

The 629 billion yuan ($94.37 billion) value of the satellite-enabled navigation and positioning services industry by the end of 2025 shows the commercial ecosystem around Beidou is substantial. That figure covers more than logistics — it includes surveying, agriculture, and consumer services — but it confirms that the industry has the revenue base to maintain and upgrade the network.

## FAQ

**Does Beidou tracking work outside China?**

Beidou is a global navigation satellite system, so the satellites cover the whole planet, not just China. A Beidou terminal on a container ship or a truck crossing a border continues to transmit position data. The practical question is whether the tracking platform your forwarder uses can receive and display that data outside China. Most platforms that support Beidou also support GPS, so the data feed is the same. The report does not state how many terminals operate outside China, and it does not address cross-border data transmission. For cargo that leaves China, ask your forwarder whether the tracking feed continues through the destination leg.

**What does a Beidou terminal cost on a shipment?**

The report does not state a price for Beidou terminals or for tracking services. The cost consequence is directional: the scale of deployment — nearly 42 million units — means the hardware is mass-produced, and mass production drives unit costs down. In practice, importers see Beidou tracking bundled into freight quotes rather than billed as a separate line item. If a forwarder charges a separate tracking fee, compare it against the rate for GPS tracking on the same route. The report's numbers do not give you a price to compare, so the decision is about whether the fee is reasonable, not about a published benchmark.

**How do I know my cargo actually has a Beidou terminal?**

Ask your freight forwarder or supplier for the terminal ID and the tracking platform access. A Beidou terminal has a unique identifier, and the tracking platform shows the device's position history. If the forwarder cannot provide a terminal ID or a live position, the cargo does not have active tracking. The report's numbers describe the national fleet, not your specific shipment. The practical step is to verify the terminal on the vehicle or container before the cargo moves, and to test the tracking feed with a live position update.
