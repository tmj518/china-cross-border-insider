## AI Summary

India's Ministry of Commerce and Industry issued its second sunset review final determination on trailer axles on 22 October 2026. The anti-dumping duty continues for five years at USD 0.16 to USD 0.31 per kilogram. Guangdong Fuwa Heavy Industries pays USD 0.16 per kilogram. Every other Chinese producer pays USD 0.31 per kilogram. The duty is charged by weight, not by value, so a heavier axle carries a larger absolute duty. Importers must price the per-kilogram amount into every landed-cost sheet for the next five years or re-source.

## What happened

On 22 October 2026, India's Ministry of Commerce and Industry published the final determination of its second sunset review of the anti-dumping duty on trailer axles (Axle for Trailers) originating in or exported from China. The determination recommends continuing the duty for five years.

The rate is set per kilogram, not as a percentage of value:

| Producer | Duty per kilogram | Duty per 1,000 kg |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guangdong Fuwa Heavy Industries Co., Ltd. | USD 0.16 | USD 160 |
| All other Chinese producers | USD 0.31 | USD 310 |

The range quoted in the determination, USD 0.16 to USD 0.31 per kilogram, is the spread between those two lines. The low end belongs to one named producer. The high end is the residual rate that applies to every other Chinese producer, whether or not that producer was examined in the review.

> **Sunset review** — a review a country runs before an anti-dumping duty expires, to decide whether removing the duty would let the dumped imports resume. A final determination that continues the measure resets the clock, so the duty runs for another fixed period rather than lapsing.

This is the second time the measure has been extended. The determination is dated 22 October 2026, and the five-year period runs from that determination.

## Why now

The review reached its final determination on 22 October 2026. A sunset review exists because the original duty had a fixed term, and the term was running out. The authority examined whether the dumping and the injury would return if the duty lapsed, and concluded that the measure continues.

The practical timing point for an importer is that the determination is dated, and the five-year continuation runs from that date. The duty does not lapse at the end of the prior period and restart later. It continues, at the rates in the table above, for the full five years from 22 October 2026.

## So what

The duty is charged per kilogram of axle weight. That single design choice decides how the cost lands on an importer, and it is the reason a percentage-based landed-cost model gives the wrong answer.

Take a consignment of trailer axles with a total shipping weight of 20,000 kilograms. If the goods are produced by Guangdong Fuwa, the duty is 20,000 × USD 0.16 = USD 3,200. If the same 20,000 kilograms come from any other Chinese producer, the duty is 20,000 × USD 0.31 = USD 6,200. The difference between the two lines on one container is USD 3,000, and it comes entirely from which factory made the axles.

Now change the weight and hold the producer constant. A 30,000-kilogram consignment from a non-Fuwa producer carries 30,000 × USD 0.31 = USD 9,300 in duty. A 10,000-kilogram consignment from the same producer carries USD 3,100. The duty scales with mass, so a heavier axle model, a heavier build, or a heavier packing method all raise the absolute duty even when the invoice value per unit is unchanged.

That is the arithmetic an importer has to run before choosing between absorbing the duty and re-sourcing. Absorbing it means adding USD 0.31 per kilogram to the landed cost of every non-Fuwa axle for five years. On a 20,000-kilogram container that is USD 6,200 per shipment, and the five-year horizon means the decision is not a one-off. Re-sourcing means finding a producer outside China, or moving volume to Guangdong Fuwa, and pricing the transition against that per-kilogram figure.

A third case decides the question for many buyers. Suppose an importer brings in 50,000 kilograms a year of axles from a non-Fuwa Chinese producer. The annual duty is 50,000 × USD 0.31 = USD 15,500. Over the five years from 22 October 2026, that is USD 77,500 in duty on the same volume, assuming weight and rate hold. If the same 50,000 kilograms came from Guangdong Fuwa, the annual duty is 50,000 × USD 0.16 = USD 8,000, and the five-year total is USD 40,000. The gap between the two producer lines over five years is USD 37,500 on 50,000 kilograms a year. That gap is the number to put against the cost of qualifying a new supplier or shifting volume to the named producer.

The per-kilogram design also means the duty does not shrink when the price of the axle falls. A percentage duty tracks the invoice value; this one does not. If the FOB price of an axle drops, the duty per kilogram stays at USD 0.16 or USD 0.31. On a low-value, heavy axle the duty becomes a larger share of the landed cost than on a high-value, light one. Importers who quote on value alone will understate the duty on their heaviest lines.

## FAQ

**Does the duty apply as a percentage of the axle's value?**

No. The determination sets the duty per kilogram of trailer axles, at USD 0.16 for Guangdong Fuwa Heavy Industries and USD 0.31 for all other Chinese producers. That means the duty is calculated on weight, not on the invoice value. A heavier axle carries a larger absolute duty even if its price is the same as a lighter one. Importers who model the duty as a percentage of FOB or CIF value will get the wrong number, and the error grows with the weight of the consignment. Build the per-kilogram figure into the landed-cost sheet directly.

**Which rate applies to my shipment?**

It depends on which producer made the axles. If the goods were produced by Guangdong Fuwa Heavy Industries Co., Ltd., the rate is USD 0.16 per kilogram. If they were produced by any other Chinese producer, the rate is USD 0.31 per kilogram. The determination names one producer at the low rate and sets the higher rate for all others. Confirm the producer named on the commercial invoice and the certificate of origin before you price the shipment, because the difference between the two lines is USD 0.15 per kilogram on every kilogram shipped.

**How long does the duty stay in force?**

Five years from the 22 October 2026 final determination. The determination is the second sunset review on this product, and it continues the measure rather than letting it lapse. For an importer, that means the per-kilogram duty is a fixed input into landed cost for the whole five-year period, not a temporary charge to be absorbed for a quarter and then forgotten. Any sourcing decision made now should assume the duty applies for the full term unless a later review changes it.

## The point

The trailer-axle duty is not a percentage that shrinks with price. It is USD 0.16 or USD 0.31 on every kilogram, for five years from 22 October 2026, and the producer named on the invoice decides which. An importer who prices the duty by weight, and who puts the five-year total against the cost of re-sourcing, makes the decision on the number that actually lands on the invoice.
