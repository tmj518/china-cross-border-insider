## AI Summary

USTR suspended its Section 301 action against China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors for two months, through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on 9 January 2027, in a notice published on 10 November 2026. The fee is deferred, not cancelled: the same action resumes unless it is further suspended. For an importer the decision that matters is the arrival date, because a voyage that ends in January falls on the other side of that line.

## What happened

The Office of the United States Trade Representative published a two-page notice on 10 November 2026 modifying the Section 301 action on China's maritime, logistics and shipbuilding sectors. Its own summary states the purpose plainly:

> "To allow for further discussion with China regarding the acts, policies, and practices at issue in this investigation, the U.S. Trade Representative is modifying the action taken in this investigation to further suspend that action for two months, until January 10, 2027."

The notice was published at 91 FR 72608, and runs to page 72609. The date line fixes the boundary: "The actions taken in this investigation are further suspended through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on January 9, 2027."

Nothing else changed. No rate was republished, no product list was rewritten, no new exemption category was created. A live action became a deferred one until a date.

## Why now

This is the second suspension on the same file, and the notice says why: further discussion with China. That is the same phrase USTR used when it extended the 178 product exclusions on the same day - two notices, published 10 November 2026, both using the date 9 January 2027 as the next decision point.

That coincidence is not decoration. It means the entire tariff relationship on this file is now governed by a single calendar date, and importers who track one but not the other will be surprised by whichever lapses first.

## So what

Three consequences, and the third is the one that costs money.

**The fee is deferred, not cancelled.** Suspension means the action does not apply during the window. It does not mean the underlying determination has been withdrawn. An operation that planned for a permanent change in the cost of moving goods on Chinese-built tonnage has planned against the wrong fact.

**The operative date is an eastern-time instant.** 11:59 p.m. on 9 January 2027 is not "9 January" in Shanghai, and it is not the sailing date. A vessel that berths on 10 January falls outside the suspension, and the clock is set in Washington, not at the port.

**Two files now share one deadline.** The product exclusions and the maritime action both run to 9 January 2027. A single review date means a single decision point, and a single failure mode: whoever tracks only the exclusions will be exposed on the maritime side on the same morning.

## How confident

**What is settled.** The suspension, its length and its end date are stated in the notice, and the notice is the primary document. The Federal Register citation is 91 FR 72608, document number 2026-22867.

**What is not settled.** Whether the action resumes on 10 January 2027. The notice suspends; it does not commit to a further suspension. Planning should assume the action returns on 10 January 2027 unless a new notice says otherwise.

**How to use this.** Put the date in the booking system as a hard boundary, not as a note. The ships that matter are the ones whose port calls land in the first ten days of January.

## For you

**If you are an importer booking ocean freight.** Ask for the port call date, not the departure date. A booking through [a freight index linked contract](/blog/container-freight-index-publisher-change-2026/) will not show you this boundary; the calendar will.

**If you are a freight forwarder or NVOCC.** Your customer's exposure changed on 10 November and will change again on 10 January. The useful service is a dated statement of which of their shipments fall on each side.

**If you are a platform seller.** You do not choose the vessel, so the only action available is to ask when the goods clear. The same logic applies to small parcels under [the low-value shipment entry rule](/blog/us-low-value-shipment-entry-rule-2026/), where the entry mechanism, not the carrier, decides the cost.

## FAQ

**Does this notice abolish the port fee?** No. It suspends the Section 301 action for two months. A suspension is a pause in application, and the notice gives no assurance beyond 9 January 2027.

**Is the deadline 9 or 10 January 2027?** The summary says "until January 10, 2027" while the date line says suspended "through 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on January 9, 2027". Those describe the same instant: the suspension covers everything up to the end of 9 January eastern time.

**Does the same date apply to the product exclusions?** Yes. USTR's separate notice on the 178 product exclusions, published the same day, extends them to 9 January 2027. Both decisions land on the same boundary.

**Which ships are affected once the suspension ends?** The action concerns Chinese-built and Chinese-operated tonnage in the maritime, logistics and shipbuilding investigation. This notice changes only the timing of its application, not its scope.

**Where is the primary document?** Federal Register document 2026-22867, 91 FR 72608, published 10 November 2026.
