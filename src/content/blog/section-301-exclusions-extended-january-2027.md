## AI Summary

USTR extended all 178 current Section 301 product exclusions from 10 November 2026 to 9 January 2027, by notice filed on 9 November 2026. The extension changes a date in the HTSUS, not a duty rate: entries after 9 January 2027 lose the exclusion, and the classification scoped by ten-digit HTSUS numbers is what decides it. An importer shipping in December should re-check the entry date against the exclusion calendar, not the invoice date.

## What happened

The Office of the United States Trade Representative filed a notice on 9 November 2026 extending all 178 of the Section 301 product exclusions that were due to expire. The operative sentence is short:

> "The Trade Representative has determined that it is appropriate to further extend all 178 current exclusions until January 10, 2027."

The mechanics are in two annexes. Annex A extends the exclusions under heading 9903.88.69 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States; Annex B extends those under heading 9903.88.70. In both, the article description changes one date: "November 9, 2026" is replaced by "January 9, 2027". The extension applies to goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on 10 November 2026 and before 11:59 p.m. eastern time on 9 January 2027.

That is the whole change. No rate moved. No product list was rewritten. A date that decides which entries are exempt.

## Why now

The exclusions have been on a rolling short extension for more than a year, and each extension has been shorter than the one before it:

- 30 May 2024: 164 exclusions reinstated through 31 May 2025 (89 FR 46948).
- 18 September 2024: 14 solar manufacturing equipment exclusions, effective 1 January 2024 (89 FR 76581).
- 5 June 2025: those 164 and 14 exclusions extended through 31 August 2025 (90 FR 23987).
- 2 September 2025: a further 90-day extension, through 29 November 2025 (90 FR 42500).
- 1 December 2025: all 178 extended through 9 November 2026 (90 FR 55232).

The pattern matters more than any single date. Each round buys a short window and leaves the next one to be announced. The current notice is consistent with that pattern: it buys roughly two months, to 9 January 2027, and the notice itself records that USTR keeps the option of further extensions or additional modifications open.

## So what

Three things follow, and only the first is obvious.

**First, the exclusion is a calendar, not a product.** The goods that benefit are defined by ten-digit HTSUS statistical reporting numbers and product descriptions in U.S. notes 20(vvv)(i) through (iv) and 20(www) to subchapter III of chapter 99. Two importers can ship the same physical item and get different outcomes if their classification differs at the tenth digit.

**Second, the entry date decides, not the order date.** The annex is explicit: the extension applies to goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse, inside a window that opens at 12:01 a.m. ET on 10 November 2026 and closes at 11:59 p.m. ET on 9 January 2027. An order placed in December that clears customs in February falls outside it.

**Third, the tariff exposure is unchanged for everything not on the list.** This notice is silent about the four tranches of additional duties imposed between 2018 and 2019 (83 FR 28719, 83 FR 40823, 83 FR 47974 as modified, and 84 FR 43304 as modified). Those remain in force. The extension does not narrow them; it only keeps a defined set of products outside them for two more months. The same arithmetic decides what a shipment lands at, which is why the method in [the landed-cost note on EU CBAM steel](/blog/eu-cbam-cost-per-tonne-chinese-steel-aluminium-2026/) applies here unchanged.

## How confident

**What is settled.** The extension, its dates, the two HTSUS headings and the ten-digit classification scope are all stated in the notice. The notice is the primary document; there is no second-hand summary needed.

**What is not settled.** Whether a further extension happens before 9 January 2027. Nothing in the notice promises one, and the prior pattern shows short extensions being granted repeatedly rather than a long one being committed. An importer planning first-quarter arrivals should treat 9 January 2027 as a real expiry, not a placeholder.

**How to use this.** Treat the exclusion as a dated permission attached to a specific HTSUS line. Confirm the line, confirm the entry date, and put the 9 January 2027 boundary into the customs broker's instructions rather than into a shared spreadsheet nobody re-reads.

## For you

**If you are an importer.** Pull the ten-digit HTSUS numbers from your last three entries, match them against U.S. notes 20(vvv) and 20(www), and write the entry-window dates into the booking instruction. The calculation to check is not the duty rate; it is the window.

**If you are a platform seller.** Your freight forwarder chooses the entry date, and you carry the duty cost. Ask for the entry summary date, not the ship date, on any shipment that will land in December or January.

**If you are a freight forwarder or broker.** This is the one place where your calendar is the product. A shipment that clears at 00:30 ET on 10 January 2027 is outside the window by thirty minutes. The parallel deadline for postal traffic is set out in [the low-value shipment entry rule](/blog/us-low-value-shipment-entry-rule-2026/), and the earlier version of this exclusion deadline is in [the November 2026 expiry note](/blog/section-301-exclusions-expire-november-2026/).

## The data point

The extension is 178 exclusions, two HTSUS headings, and a window that runs from 12:01 a.m. ET on 10 November 2026 to 11:59 p.m. ET on 9 January 2027.

## FAQ

**Does this notice reduce the Section 301 duty?** No. It extends the period during which 178 specific products are excluded from the additional duties. The rates applied to products outside those exclusions are untouched.

**What happens on 10 January 2027 if nothing further is announced?** The exclusions lapse and entries after 9 January 2027 fall outside the window stated in the annexes. The notice keeps the door open to further extensions, but none is granted by this document.

**Is the exclusion based on the product description or the HTSUS number?** Both, and the scope is governed by the ten-digit statistical reporting number together with the product description in the U.S. notes. A description match with a different tenth digit is not the same exclusion.

**Does the date refer to when the goods ship or when they clear customs?** When they are entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption. A vessel that sails in December and clears in January is judged on the January entry.

**Where is the primary document?** The notice is filed at FR Doc. 2026-22925, published 10 November 2026, signed by the General Counsel of USTR.
