## AI Summary

Brazil's country-specific beef safeguard quota reached 100% on 29 September 2026. Under the rule set by MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025, the surcharge starts on the third day from that trigger: 00:00 on 1 October 2026. From that moment, Brazilian-origin beef pays a 55% additional tariff on top of the applicable MFN rate. The surcharge is a percentage of value, so it scales with the CIF price of every consignment. Importers holding Brazilian beef that has not yet been declared need to reprice the cargo or reroute it before the next quota cycle fills.

## What happened

The Ministry of Commerce Trade Remedy Bureau issued notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号 on 30 September 2026. The notice states that beef imported from Brazil under the beef safeguard measure reached 100% of the country-specific quantity set out in MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025 on 29 September 2026.

The consequence is written into the same notice. From the third day after the 100% trigger — from 00:00 on 1 October 2026 — beef imported from Brazil carries a 55% additional tariff levied on top of the applicable tariff rate in force.

Three elements of that sentence carry the cost:

- **The base.** The 55% is added to the applicable MFN rate, not substituted for it. The total duty on a Brazilian beef line is the MFN rate plus 55 percentage points.
- **The trigger.** The quota is measured by quantity, and it is measured per country. Brazil's allocation is the one that filled.
- **The clock.** The notice counts days from the trigger, not from publication. The notice was published on 30 September 2026, one day before the surcharge began.

> **Additional tariff under a safeguard** — a percentage charge applied to the value of imported goods on top of the ordinary customs duty, activated when a country's allocated import quantity under a safeguard measure is used up. It is calculated on the customs value of the consignment, so a higher-priced cut carries a higher surcharge in absolute money than a cheaper cut of the same weight.

The instrument that sets the quota and the surcharge rule is MOFCOM Announcement No. 87 of 2025. The 30 September notice does not create the 55% rate; it records that the condition for applying it has been met for Brazil and fixes the start time.

## Why now

The quota did not fill early in the year. It filled at the end of September, which is the point in the calendar when the 2026 safeguard year is close to running out and when shipping programmes for the fourth quarter are already booked.

The notice gives no warning period beyond the rule itself. The third-day count is fixed in Announcement No. 87 of 2025, so the start date was determined the moment the quota crossed 100% on 29 September. Publication followed on 30 September, and the surcharge began at midnight the next day. An importer reading the notice on the day it appeared had one working day of notice.

That is the whole of the timing story. The quota filled, the rule converted the fill date into a start date, and the notice recorded both.

## So what

The judgement is simple: from 1 October 2026, the landed cost of Brazilian-origin beef into China carries a 55% charge that is proportional to value and applies to the whole consignment, not to the portion above any threshold. There is no partial relief in the notice. The surcharge is not a quota rent that a buyer can negotiate around; it is a duty line on the entry.

The arithmetic is a multiplication, and it is worth running on a real consignment rather than in the abstract. Take a CIF value of USD 100,000 for a container of Brazilian beef. The additional tariff is 55% of that value: USD 55,000. Add the applicable MFN duty on the same value and that is the total duty bill. The surcharge alone is more than half the goods value.

Scale it across three consignment sizes:

| CIF value (USD) | 55% additional tariff (USD) | Total duty at an illustrative 12% MFN (USD) | Duty as share of CIF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50,000 | 27,500 | 33,500 | 67% |
| 100,000 | 55,000 | 67,000 | 67% |
| 250,000 | 137,500 | 167,500 | 67% |

The MFN figure in that table is illustrative only, used to show how the two charges stack. The 55% column is the number the notice supplies, and it is the one to carry into a repricing sheet.

Three worked cases show where the decision sits.

**Case one: cargo not yet declared.** A consignment of Brazilian beef that has arrived but has not had its customs declaration accepted before 00:00 on 1 October 2026 pays the surcharge. The cost of clearing is now CIF value times 55% plus the MFN duty. The choice is to absorb it, renegotiate the price with the Brazilian supplier against the new landed cost, or divert the cargo to another market. Absorbing it means the margin on that container is gone unless the resale price moves.

**Case two: cargo booked for the next quota cycle.** A buyer planning Brazilian beef arrivals later in the safeguard year is buying into a market where the country quota is already at 100%. Every subsequent Brazilian consignment in the same safeguard year carries the 55% add-on. The forward price a supplier quotes needs to be tested against that: a quote that assumes the pre-surcharge duty is understating the landed cost by 55 percentage points of CIF.

**Case three: origin substitution.** A buyer who switches to a non-Brazilian origin avoids the country-specific surcharge, because the trigger in the notice is tied to Brazil's allocation. That switch has its own costs — supplier qualification, plant registration, shipping lead time — and those costs need to be compared against 55% of CIF on the volume in question. On a USD 100,000 container, the surcharge avoided is USD 55,000, which is the budget available for the switch before it stops paying.

The comparison to make is between the surcharge and the cost of the alternative, consignment by consignment. The surcharge is knowable in advance: it is 55% of the declared value. The alternative cost is not, which is why the arithmetic should be done before the next booking rather than after the next arrival.

## How confident

**Confirmed.** The notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号, published by the MOFCOM Trade Remedy Bureau on 30 September 2026, states that Brazilian beef reached 100% of the country-specific quantity under Announcement No. 87 of 2025 on 29 September 2026, and that from the third day — 00:00 on 1 October 2026 — a 55% additional tariff applies on top of the applicable rate. The trigger date, the day count, the rate, the start time and the rule-setting instrument number all come from that single document.

**Inferred.** Everything beyond those five facts rests on that one notice. The notice does not state the applicable MFN rate for any beef line, so the total-duty column in the table above uses an illustrative 12% purely to show how the two charges stack; it is not a rate taken from any document. The notice does not address in-transit cargo, goods in bonded storage, or declarations accepted before the deadline but released after it. The treatment of those cases is not stated, and this article does not assume one. The statement that a switch to another origin avoids the surcharge follows from the notice tying the trigger to Brazil's allocation, but no second document confirms how other countries' allocations stand.

**What would change my mind.** A subsequent MOFCOM notice or customs guidance that sets out transitional treatment for cargo already on the water, or that clarifies which timestamp — declaration acceptance, release, or arrival — governs the surcharge, would change the operational advice in the "For you" section. A notice recording that another country's allocation has also reached 100% would change the origin-substitution case, because the alternative origin would then carry its own surcharge. And a published MFN rate for the specific beef lines in question would replace the illustrative figure in the table with a real total-duty number.

## FAQ

**Does the 55% apply to the whole consignment or only to the volume above the quota?**

It applies to the whole consignment. The notice records that Brazil's country-specific quantity reached 100% and that from the third day a 55% additional tariff applies to beef imported from Brazil. There is no wording in the notice that limits the surcharge to a portion of a shipment or to a quantity above a threshold. Once the country allocation is exhausted, the surcharge is a duty line on the entry, calculated on the customs value of the goods declared. An importer should treat the full CIF value of each Brazilian beef consignment as the base for the 55% calculation.

**Which date decides whether my cargo pays the surcharge?**

The notice fixes the start at 00:00 on 1 October 2026, the third day counted from the 29 September 2026 trigger. The notice does not state which customs event — declaration acceptance, release, or vessel arrival — is the operative timestamp. Importers should treat the declaration acceptance time as the working assumption and confirm the treatment of their specific cargo with their broker. Cargo declared before the deadline sits on one side of the line; cargo declared after it sits on the other, and the difference is 55% of CIF.

**Does switching to a non-Brazilian origin avoid the surcharge?**

The trigger in the notice is tied to Brazil's country-specific allocation, so a consignment of beef originating elsewhere is not caught by this particular surcharge. That does not make the switch free. Supplier qualification, plant registration and shipping lead time all carry cost, and those costs need to be compared against 55% of CIF on the volume being moved. The notice does not state the status of other countries' allocations, so an importer should confirm the position for the intended alternative origin before committing volume to it.
