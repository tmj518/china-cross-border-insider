## AI Summary

Brazil used 100% of its beef safeguard allocation on 29 September 2026. From 00:00 on 1 October 2026, every Brazilian beef shipment pays a 55% additional tariff on top of the current applicable duty rate. The add-on is a percentage of value, so it scales with the cargo, not with the tonnage over the line. An importer holding Brazilian beef that clears on or after that timestamp prices the 55% into the landed cost before the container is sold.

## What happened

The MOFCOM Trade Remedy Investigation Bureau issued notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号 on 30 September 2026, the ninth execution notice in the 2026 beef safeguard series. It records two facts and one consequence.

The facts: under the beef safeguard, imports of Brazilian beef reached 100% of the country quantity set in MOFCOM Announcement 2025 No. 87 on 29 September 2026. The consequence: from the third day after that 100% mark — 00:00 on 1 October 2026 — beef imported from Brazil carries a 55% additional tariff on top of the current applicable duty rate.

The notice's own wording is the binding text:

> 在巴西牛肉进口数量达到规定数量 100% 的第 3 日起（ 10 月 1 日零点起），自该国进口牛肉将在现行适用关税税率基础上加征 55% 关税。

Two things about that sentence matter for pricing. First, the add-on is calculated **on the current applicable duty rate base** — it is an additional tariff, not a replacement rate. Second, the trigger is country-specific: the notice names Brazil, and the 100% figure is Brazil's share of its own allocation, not the global safeguard volume.

> **Country allocation** — the portion of a safeguard quota assigned to one supplying country. When that country's imports reach 100% of its allocation, the safeguard's additional tariff switches on for that origin alone. Other origins keep importing against their own allocations until each one fills.

The notice does not state the size of Brazil's allocation in tonnes, does not state the current applicable duty rate for beef, and does not address cargo already on the water on 1 October. Those three gaps are where the importer's work sits.

## Why now

The exhaustion date is the whole story. Brazil's allocation ran out on 29 September 2026, two days before the add-on started. The notice was issued on 30 September, one day before the start time. An importer reading the notice on the day it was published had one day of notice before the 55% began to apply.

That is not a criticism of the notice — it is the design of the measure. Announcement 2025 No. 87 set the country quantities and the 55% add-on in advance; the execution notice only reports when a country crosses 100%. The trigger is arithmetic, not discretion. The volume arrived, the percentage was met, the clock ran three days, and the rate changed.

For anyone sourcing beef into China, the practical effect is that Brazil moved from "inside the safeguard" to "outside the safeguard" on a date that was visible only in the import statistics, not in a scheduled calendar.

## So what

The 55% is a value tax, and that changes how it hits a container.

Take a consignment declared at a CIF value of USD 100,000. The add-on is USD 55,000. On a USD 250,000 consignment it is USD 137,500. The add-on does not care how many tonnes are in the box or how far over the allocation the shipment pushed the total. It applies to the declared value of the goods, on top of whatever the current applicable duty rate already produces.

| Declared CIF value (USD) | 55% add-on (USD) | Total duty if current rate is 12% (USD) | Total duty if current rate is 0% (USD) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100,000 | 55,000 | 67,000 | 55,000 |
| 250,000 | 137,500 | 167,500 | 137,500 |
| 500,000 | 275,000 | 335,000 | 275,000 |

The middle column is the one to read carefully. The 55% is not 55% of the existing duty; it is 55% of value, stacked on top of the existing duty. At a 12% current rate, a USD 250,000 consignment moves from USD 30,000 of duty to USD 167,500. The add-on is more than four times the duty it sits on.

Three decisions follow from that arithmetic.

**Clear before the timestamp, or price after it.** The notice sets the start at 00:00 on 1 October 2026. A shipment that completes its customs declaration before that moment sits outside the add-on as the notice describes it. A shipment that declares after it pays the 55%. The gap between those two outcomes on a USD 250,000 consignment is USD 137,500 — larger than most importers' entire margin on the container.

**Re-run the landed cost, not the FOB price.** A supplier quoting FOB Santos has no reason to move the price, because the 55% is collected at the Chinese border, not at the Brazilian port. The landed cost changes even when the invoice does not. Any margin calculation built on the old landed cost is now wrong by 55% of CIF.

**Check the other allocations before switching origin.** The notice covers Brazil. Announcement 2025 No. 87 assigns quantities by country, and each country fills on its own schedule. An origin that still has allocation room imports without the 55% add-on; an origin that has also crossed 100% does not. The switch is only worth making if the destination allocation is open, and that status has to be checked against the latest execution notice in the （九） series, not assumed from the fact that the country is not Brazil.

A fourth case is worth working because it is the one importers get wrong. Suppose a Brazilian consignment is split across two declarations, one accepted on 30 September and one on 2 October, on a total CIF of USD 400,000 split evenly. The first half, USD 200,000, clears under the pre-trigger rules. The second half pays USD 110,000 in add-on. Splitting the declaration does not split the add-on proportionally across the whole cargo — it applies to whatever value is declared after the timestamp. The only lever is the declaration time of each entry, and the notice gives no basis for treating a single commercial shipment as one unit for this purpose.

## How confident

**Confirmed.** The notice 商救济进一函〔2026〕58号 states that Brazilian beef reached 100% of its Announcement 2025 No. 87 country quantity on 29 September 2026, and that from 00:00 on 1 October 2026 Brazilian beef carries a 55% additional tariff on top of the current applicable duty rate. The notice number, the issue date of 30 September 2026, and the （九） sequence number are all on the document.

**Inferred.** Everything about the size of the cost rests on the notice's own formula and on the declared value of a shipment, which the notice does not contain. The notice does not state Brazil's allocation in tonnes, does not state the current applicable duty rate for beef, and does not say whether cargo loaded before 1 October but declared after it is treated differently. The worked figures in this article are arithmetic on assumed CIF values, not figures from the document. **What would change my mind.** A later execution notice in the same series showing Brazil's allocation reopened, a customs ruling treating pre-1 October loading as outside the add-on, or a published statement of the current applicable duty rate for the beef lines in question. Any of those would move the numbers in the table. Absent them, the notice's formula is the only basis for pricing.

## FAQ

**Does the 55% apply to the whole shipment or only to the volume above the allocation?**

It applies to the whole shipment. The notice sets the add-on as 55% on top of the current applicable duty rate for beef imported from Brazil from 00:00 on 1 October 2026. It does not describe a partial rate for the first tranche of a consignment or a split between in-quota and over-quota volume. Once Brazil's allocation reached 100% on 29 September 2026, the country-level trigger was met, and the add-on attaches to Brazilian beef as an origin rather than to a quantity within a shipment. The practical consequence is that the cost scales with the declared value of each entry, not with how far the shipment pushed the total over the line.

**Is the 55% calculated on the existing duty or on the value of the goods?**

On the value of the goods, added to the existing duty. The notice's wording is that the 55% is levied 在现行适用关税税率基础上 — on top of the current applicable duty rate. That means the current rate is calculated as it always was, and the 55% is calculated on the same value base and added to it. On a consignment declared at USD 250,000 with a current rate of 12%, the existing duty is USD 30,000 and the add-on is USD 137,500, for a total of USD 167,500. The add-on is not 55% of the USD 30,000.

**Do other countries' beef allocations still have room?**

The notice does not say. It reports Brazil's position only, and it does not list the status of any other country's allocation under Announcement 2025 No. 87. The only reliable way to answer the question for a specific origin is to read the latest execution notice in the （九） series for that country. Treating a non-Brazilian origin as open because it is not named in this notice is an assumption, not a finding. Before committing to a switch, confirm the destination country's allocation status against the most recent notice in the series.

```

Total duty = (declared CIF value x current applicable rate) + add-on

Case A: CIF 100,000, current rate 12%

  add-on        = 100,000 x 0.55 = 55,000
```

## The point

The Brazil trigger is not a forecast and not a risk to be monitored. It has already happened: 100% on 29 September 2026, add-on from 00:00 on 1 October 2026. Every Brazilian beef entry declared after that timestamp carries 55% of its CIF value as extra duty, on top of whatever the current rate produces. The importer's job is not to decide whether the add-on applies but to know, entry by entry, which side of the timestamp each declaration falls on — and to check the other allocations before assuming a switch of origin solves the problem.
