fix: refresh the article whose deadline is 15 days out, and two gaps it had

Round one of standing instruction C — no external dependencies, improve what is
already published — applied where it has a deadline. The de minimis postal article
carries a reviewBy of 22 October, which is 15 days away, and 22 October is the date
its central fact takes effect. So it was re-read against the rule itself rather than
left until the date passed.

The rule is 91 FR 37801, published 24 June 2026, and it is already the article's
cited source — so this was a re-read, not new research. The core facts held: the
compliance date of 22 October 2026, the $2,500 ceiling, HTSUS chapters 1 to 97, and
the indefinite suspension of the $800 postal exemption. Two things did not.

First, the article described only half the date. The 22 October compliance date
covers 19 CFR 145.12(a)(2)(v) as well as (vi). Subparagraph (vi) is the Chapter 98
and 99 exclusion the article covered; subparagraph (v) excludes merchandise subject
to another government agency, which reaches product categories carrying no Chapter 99
code at all. A seller whose SKUs are not Section 301 goods is not thereby clear.

Second, the article treated the bond as the main new requirement and said a shipment
needs "a broker or a filing process". The rule is harder than that. The International
Mail Duty Worksheet requires all applicable 10-digit HTSUS classifications, and CBP
concluded that submitting it is customs business — so where the filer is not the
owner or purchaser of the goods, the filing has to come from a licensed customs
broker. CBP's own estimate in the rule is that roughly half the parties qualified
under the interim process are not licensed brokers, and every one of them is excluded
from 22 October. Foreign postal operators filing on a seller's behalf are in that
group. That is an eligibility wall rather than a cost line, and it is the finding
worth correcting.
