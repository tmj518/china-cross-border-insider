## What happened

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) published a domestic refined oil price adjustment notice on 12 November 2026. The notice states that, from 24:00 on 12 November 2026, the retail prices of standard-grade gasoline and diesel are cut by 645 yuan per tonne and 625 yuan per tonne respectively. The previous adjustment took effect on 29 October 2026. The new cut follows a ten-working-day averaging window in which the average price of international crude oil fell below the average of the ten working days before the previous adjustment.

The notice also directs consumers to report price violations through the 12315 platform. This is the standard enforcement channel attached to NDRC fuel price adjustments.

> **Standard-grade refined oil** — the benchmark gasoline and diesel grades against which the NDRC sets its retail price adjustments. The 645 and 625 yuan per tonne cuts apply to these standard grades; other grades are adjusted proportionally by the same mechanism.

The notice does not state the prior or new ceiling price levels. It states only the size of the cut. Any calculation of a new absolute price per litre would require a baseline the notice does not supply.

## Why now

The NDRC adjusts domestic refined oil prices on a fixed cycle. The mechanism compares the average international crude price over the ten working days before the current adjustment with the average over the ten working days before the previous adjustment. The previous adjustment was on 29 October 2026. Since then, international crude prices fell, which produced the downward adjustment effective 12 November 2026.

The notice itself states that international crude prices fluctuated and fell after the 29 October adjustment. The ten-working-day averaging window is the instrument that converts that international movement into a domestic retail price change. The cut is the direct result of that comparison.

## So what

For an importer, the relevant question is what a 645 yuan per tonne gasoline cut and a 625 yuan per tonne diesel cut mean for the cost of moving goods inside China. The answer depends on three variables: the fuel type the truck uses, the distance travelled, and the fuel consumption rate of the vehicle.

The NDRC notice does not supply a consumption rate or a density for diesel. The arithmetic below therefore uses a worked example with stated assumptions, not figures from the notice. The reader can substitute their own consumption and distance figures.

Consider a standard 40-foot container moving from a factory in the Pearl River Delta to a port in Shenzhen, a distance of roughly 80 kilometres. A heavy truck on that route consumes approximately 30 litres of diesel per 100 kilometres. At a density of 0.84 kilograms per litre, that is 25.2 kilograms of diesel per 100 kilometres, or 20.16 kilograms for the 80-kilometre leg.

The diesel cut is 625 yuan per tonne, or 0.625 yuan per kilogram. The saving on this leg is therefore 20.16 kilograms multiplied by 0.625 yuan per kilogram, which equals 12.6 yuan. That is a small but real reduction in the factory-gate-to-port leg.

For a longer move, the saving scales linearly. A 1,500-kilometre move from a factory in Chongqing to the port of Shanghai consumes 450 litres of diesel, or 378 kilograms. The saving is 378 multiplied by 0.625, which equals 236.25 yuan. On a container with a landed value of 200,000 yuan, that is roughly 0.12 percent of the cargo value.

The same logic applies to gasoline. A light van used for last-mile delivery, consuming 10 litres of gasoline per 100 kilometres, saves 0.084 yuan per kilometre after the 645 yuan per tonne cut, assuming a gasoline density of 0.73 kilograms per litre. A 200-kilometre delivery run saves 16.8 yuan.

The saving is not a reason to renegotiate a freight contract. It is a reason to check that a carrier's fuel surcharge is being adjusted downward. Many trucking contracts include a fuel adjustment clause tied to published fuel prices. The NDRC cut is the published signal that triggers such clauses.

For importers who quote delivered prices to their own customers, the cut reduces the delivered cost slightly. ## FAQ

**Does the 645 and 625 yuan per tonne cut apply to all gasoline and diesel grades?**

The NDRC notice specifies that the cut applies to standard-grade gasoline and diesel. Other grades are adjusted proportionally by the same mechanism, but the notice does not state the exact amounts for those grades. In practice, provincial pricing bureaus publish the adjusted prices for each grade after the NDRC announcement. An importer buying a non-standard grade should check the provincial notice for the exact figure rather than assume the 645 or 625 yuan per tonne amount applies directly.

**How do I calculate the fuel saving on my specific shipment?**

The saving equals the fuel consumed multiplied by the cut per kilogram. For diesel, the cut is 625 yuan per tonne, or 0.625 yuan per kilogram. Multiply the distance by the consumption rate in litres per 100 kilometres, convert litres to kilograms using the fuel density, and multiply by the cut per kilogram. For example, a 1,500-kilometre move at 30 litres per 100 kilometres uses 450 litres; at 0.84 kilograms per litre that is 378 kilograms; multiplied by 0.625 yuan per kilogram, the saving is 236.25 yuan. Substitute your own distance, consumption, and density figures.

**What should I do if my carrier does not reduce its fuel surcharge?**

The NDRC notice directs consumers to report price violations through the 12315 platform. If your freight contract includes a fuel adjustment clause tied to published fuel prices, the NDRC cut is the trigger for a downward adjustment. Send the notice to your carrier and request the recalculation in writing. If the carrier refuses, the 12315 platform is the designated channel for reporting the violation. The notice does not specify a penalty for non-compliance, but it does establish the reporting mechanism.
