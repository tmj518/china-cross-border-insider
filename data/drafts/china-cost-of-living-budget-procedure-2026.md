## Definition

This article sets out the procedure for building a defensible cost-of-living budget for a stay in China when no official page publishes a single cost-of-living figure, and it applies to anyone relocating staff, pricing a long assignment, or costing a sourcing trip against a Chinese budget line.

## Why there is no single number to quote

The question "what is the cost of living in China" has no official answer, and that is not an oversight. Chinese statistical releases are built as indices and averages across a national territory, not as a household budget for a named city. A national average blends coastal cities and inland provinces, so it describes no place a person actually lives.

The practical consequence for a budget is that you assemble the figure yourself, from published components, and you record where each component came from. The rest of this article is that assembly procedure.

## Steps

1. Fix the scope before you look for any figure: name the city, the number of people, the length of the stay, and whether housing is provided. A budget without a named city cannot be checked against any published series.
2. Collect the official price index releases for the province or city in scope, and record the release date and the base period of each series. An index tells you how prices moved; it does not tell you what a household spends.
3. Collect the official average wage or income release for the same geography and the same period. This is the anchor most published budgets are built on, and it is the figure a reviewer will ask you to justify.
4. Separate the budget into the categories the official series themselves use, so that each line of your budget maps to a published category rather than to your own grouping.
5. Price the categories the official series do not cover — housing deposits, school fees, imported goods, and anything you buy at a foreign price — from invoices, quotations, or the provider's own published rate card, and keep those documents.
6. Convert every line to a single currency and a single period, and state the exchange rate and its date on the face of the budget.
7. Write the assumptions page last: what you took from an official release, what you took from a quotation, and what you estimated. An estimate that is labelled is usable; an estimate that is dressed as an official figure is not.

## Who this route is for

Anyone can build a budget this way. There is no application, no eligibility test, and no office that approves a cost-of-living figure. The route is open because it is a research procedure, not a filing.

## What documents are required

The documents are the ones you gather, not the ones you submit. Keep the official release you took each index or wage figure from, with its date. Keep the quotation or invoice behind every line you did not take from an official release. Keep the exchange rate source and its date. If the budget supports a visa, a work permit, or a payroll decision, the reviewer will ask for these, and a budget that cannot produce them is treated as unsupported.

## Which conditions stop it

The procedure fails in a few recognisable ways. It fails when the geography in the budget does not match the geography of the release — a national index standing in for a city. It fails when the period does not match — an annual release used for a monthly budget without saying so. It fails when a category is priced from a source that is not the provider of the good. And it fails when the budget is presented as an official figure rather than as your own assembly.

## The usual reasons a budget is sent back

Budgets come back because the assumptions page is missing, because a line has no source behind it, because the exchange rate is undated, or because the scope changed after the figures were collected and the budget was not rebuilt. The most common reason is the first: the person who built it knew where every number came from, and did not write it down.

## How confident

- **What we know:** The official pages consulted for this article — the National Immigration Administration statistics index, the Ministry of Education English portal, the State Council English portal, and the National Bureau of Statistics English release index — were retrieved on 2026-10-30 and none of them published a cost-of-living figure for a household. The National Bureau of Statistics English portal presents its releases under headings for data, communiqués, and latest releases, which is where an index or wage series is found rather than a household budget.
- **What we do not know:** We do not know the current value of any price index, wage figure, or exchange rate, because no page carrying those values was retrieved for this article. We do not state a cost of living for any city, and this article deliberately contains no amount, rate, or threshold.
- **How to check:** Go to the National Bureau of Statistics English portal and open the latest releases and communiqués sections for the index and wage series covering your city or province, and record the release date of each. For the documents an importer needs when moving goods or staff, start with our [importing from China documents checklist](/blog/importing-from-china-documents-checklist), which this article extends from a budget rather than a shipment angle.

## What to do next

Build the scope sheet first, before you open any statistical release. A budget whose city, period, and household size are fixed can be checked line by line; a budget that starts from a national average cannot be checked at all, and will be rebuilt from scratch the first time someone asks where a figure came from.
