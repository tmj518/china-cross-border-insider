## Definition

A China sourcing trip is a buyer's travel to the mainland to meet suppliers in person, inspect production, and decide whether to place a first order — and this article sets out the order of the steps that a buyer can state from official sources, not the price of the trip.

## Why there is no fee figure here

The questions behind this page are commercial ones: what a sourcing agent charges, what a trip costs, and how to arrange a factory visit. Those are prices set between private parties. The Ministry of Commerce, the State Council portal and the National Immigration Administration publish the rules that govern entry and company registration; they do not publish what an agent charges or what a hotel costs. This article therefore does not quote a fee, a commission, a tour price or a number of days. It sets out the sequence, the documents, and the points where a trip goes wrong — all of which a buyer can act on before booking anything.

## Steps

1. **Establish the basis on which you enter.** Decide the purpose of the trip before the itinerary, and confirm which entry category that purpose falls under with the National Immigration Administration. A trip arranged as tourism and then used for factory negotiation is a mismatch between stated purpose and actual activity.
2. **Decide who you are actually meeting.** Establish from documents — not from a website — whether the counterparty is a factory or a trading company, using the verification order in the linked article below. Run this before the trip, not during it.
3. **Fix what the visit is for, in writing.** State in advance what the visit must prove: the production line, the registered name on the premises, the quality-control step, and the documents the supplier will issue. A visit with a pass-or-fail list is worth more than an impression.
4. **Separate the agent's role from the supplier's.** Make clear which functions the agent performs — vetting, negotiation, inspection, consolidation, export paperwork — and keep the agent's fee as a separate stated line rather than folded into the unit price.
5. **Collect the documents before you leave.** Take back the counterparty's registration details as verified on site, the agreed specification and sample, and the identity of the entity that will issue the export documents, so the shipment does not have to be re-arranged by email.

## Step one: establish the basis on which you enter

The first decision is not which factory to visit but on what basis you are travelling. A business visit and a tourist visit are different entry categories, and the National Immigration Administration is the body that publishes the entry rules and the visa categories. Before any supplier is contacted, confirm which category your trip falls under and what that category requires of you. A trip arranged as tourism and then used for factory negotiation is a mismatch between the stated purpose and the actual activity, and it is the kind of mismatch that surfaces at the border rather than at the factory gate.

The practical consequence: decide the purpose of the trip first, then let the itinerary follow. Do not book flights and then look for a category that fits them.

## Step two: decide who you are actually meeting

The single most common way a sourcing trip wastes its cost is that the buyer spends it with a trading company while believing it is a factory. A trading company is a legitimate counterparty, but it is a different one: it sits between you and production, it carries its own margin, and it does not control the line. Before you travel, establish which of the two you are going to see, and do it from documents rather than from a website.

The verification order — the business licence, the registered scope of business, the registered address against the claimed plant address, and the real-name registration record — is set out in full in [how to tell a factory from a trading company before you wire a deposit](/blog/how-to-tell-a-factory-from-a-trading-company/). Run that check before the trip, not during it. A trip that discovers on arrival that the "factory" is an office has already spent its budget.

## Step three: fix what the visit is for, in writing

A factory visit has a purpose beyond looking around: it is the point at which claims made by email are tested against the physical operation. State in advance what you are going to check, so the visit has a pass or fail rather than an impression. The items that a buyer can verify on site, and that a supplier cannot easily stage, are:

- **The production line itself** — whether the process you are buying is actually run at that address, or whether the address is a warehouse or a trading office.
- **The registered name on the premises** — the entity named on the business licence against the sign, the contract counterparty and the bank account that will receive the deposit. These should be the same entity.
- **The quality-control step** — where in the line inspection happens, who performs it, and whether it is in-house or subcontracted.
- **The documents the supplier will issue** — the invoice, the packing list and the origin documents, and whether the entity issuing them is the entity you are paying.

If a supplier resists a visit to the production site, that resistance is itself the finding. It is information about the counterparty, and it is cheaper to act on before the deposit than after.

## Step four: separate the agent's role from the supplier's

A sourcing agent is a service provider, and the trip should make clear which functions the agent performs and which the supplier performs. The functions that matter are: identifying and vetting the supplier, negotiating the price, inspecting during production, consolidating shipments, and handling the export paperwork. An agent that performs the vetting but not the inspection is a different service from one that does both, and the trip is the point at which you can see which one you have engaged.

The rule that keeps this clean: the entity you pay the deposit to and the entity that issues the commercial documents should be the same, and the agent's own fee should be a separate, stated line rather than folded into the unit price. If the agent will not separate them, you cannot tell what the goods cost from what the service cost.

## Step five: what to bring back

The trip produces documents, not memories. Before you leave, collect the items that the later import steps depend on: the counterparty's registration details as verified on site, the specification and sample agreed, and the identity of the entity that will issue the export documents. The full list of what a shipment needs at the border is in [the documents that decide whether the container moves](/blog/importing-from-china-documents-checklist/), and a trip that returns without the supplier-side half of that list has to be repeated by email.

## What blocks a trip, and why applications are sent back

The reasons a sourcing trip fails are procedural rather than commercial, and they repeat:

- **Entry on the wrong basis.** The stated purpose of the visit does not match what the traveller intends to do, which is a border problem, not a factory problem.
- **A visit to an address that is not the production site.** The counterparty is real but is not the manufacturer, and the price reflects a margin the buyer did not know was there.
- **A deposit paid to an entity other than the one on the licence.** The money leaves before the mismatch is noticed.
- **No written record of what was agreed on site.** The specification discussed in the meeting is not the specification in the contract.
- **Documents collected from the agent rather than the supplier.** The issuing entity on the export paperwork is not the one the buyer verified.

Each of these is avoidable by ordering the steps as above: entry basis first, counterparty identity second, visit purpose third, agent role fourth, documents last.

## How confident

- **What we know:** The National Immigration Administration publishes the entry rules and visa categories for travel to the mainland, and the State Council portal carries the government's published notices; both were retrieved on 2026-11-03. The verification order for distinguishing a factory from a trading company, and the document list a shipment needs at the border, are set out in the two linked articles on this site.
- **What we do not know:** No official source retrieved for this article publishes a sourcing-agent fee, a commission rate, a tour price, or a recommended number of days for a trip. Those are commercial terms set between private parties, and this article states none of them. The entry category that applies to a given traveller depends on that traveller's purpose and nationality, which the National Immigration Administration page must be read against directly.
- **How to check:** Read the entry rules at the National Immigration Administration (nia.gov.cn, and its English site en.nia.gov.cn) for the category that matches your purpose of travel; check the counterparty's registration through the State Administration for Market Regulation real-name registration system described in the linked factory-verification article; and confirm the export-document list against the customs announcement cited in the linked documents article.

## The one-line version

The trip is not the cost — the order of the steps is. Establish the entry basis, verify who you are meeting, fix what the visit must prove, keep the agent's fee separate from the goods price, and leave with documents rather than impressions.
