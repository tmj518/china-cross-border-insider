## AI Summary

China's Ministry of Commerce published a revised dual-use export control list on 18 November 2026, and a joint ministry consultation proposes 40 new restricted technologies, 12 of them in photovoltaics. Because China holds 76.2% to 96.4% of global solar capacity across the four main stages, a buyer of Chinese-origin solar goods faces a licence gate rather than a tariff.

## What happened

The Ministry of Commerce posted a revised dual-use export control list on its website on 18 November 2026. The list is the reference document an exporter checks a product against to decide whether a licence is needed before the goods leave China.

The same week, the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Science and Technology opened a consultation on additions to the separate catalogue of technologies whose export is prohibited or restricted. That proposal names 40 technologies for addition, spread across artificial intelligence, biomedicine and photovoltaics. Of those 40, 12 sit in the photovoltaic field, covering stages that include polysilicon manufacturing, wafer manufacturing and cell manufacturing.

## Why now

The list revision and the technology consultation landed in the same week, which puts two separate gates in front of the same shipment. A buyer who classified an order against the previous list now has a new reference document to check against, and a buyer sourcing solar manufacturing technology now has a named list of 40 items under consultation.

## So what

The 12 photovoltaic technologies are not a marginal corner of the global supply chain. According to China Photovoltaic Industry Association capacity data for 2025, cited in the same trade report, China held 95.3% of global polysilicon capacity, 96.4% of wafer capacity, 90.3% of cell capacity and 76.2% of module capacity.

Read against the proposed additions, those shares tell a buyer where the exposure sits. The three upstream stages named in the consultation - polysilicon, wafer and cell - are the stages where China's share of global capacity runs from 90.3% to 96.4%. A module assembler outside China that sources wafers or cells has few alternative suppliers at those volumes. The downstream module stage, at 76.2%, is the most diversified of the four, but it is still a majority of world capacity.

The cost consequence is not a tariff rate. It is a licence gate: a shipment of a listed technology or a good incorporating it waits for an export licence, and the delay is the cost. The documents do not state a licence processing time, so this article does not put a number on it.

## FAQ

**Does the 18 November list impose a tariff?** No. It is a control list, not a duty schedule. It decides whether a licence is needed, not what the goods cost at the border.

**Are the 40 technologies already restricted?** No. They are proposed for addition and are under consultation. The list itself was published on 18 November 2026; the 40 additions are a separate proposal.

**Which solar stages are named?** The consultation names polysilicon manufacturing, wafer manufacturing and cell manufacturing among the 12 photovoltaic technologies.
