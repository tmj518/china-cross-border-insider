feat: article 21, the supply-chain piece, and the three things the draft had wrong

Publishes #21 in supply-chain, the category that had two pieces and neither of them
this week. Fills the skeleton that had been sitting since 28 September — and the
skeleton was not salvageable, which is the reason this is a rewrite rather than an
edit.

Checking its claims against the release found three defects:

The draft said August industrial profits rose 15.7 per cent. 15.7 per cent is the
January-to-August cumulative figure; August alone grew 4.2 per cent. The draft
attributed 21.3 per cent growth to equipment manufacturing; the release has
general-purpose equipment at 2.5 per cent and special-purpose equipment at minus 2.1
per cent — the opposite direction. And it cited 6.2 per cent container throughput
growth, a number with no source that appears nowhere in the release.

The draft also carried a first-person story about a 2021 pricing mistake. The spec
bans that form precisely because zero-human-review makes it unverifiable, and this is
what it looks like when it slips in.

Worse, the thesis was backwards. The draft argued that recovering profits make
Chinese suppliers hold firmer prices. The data says the opposite is happening at the
same time: receivables now take 72.2 days to collect, up 0.9 days, and finished-goods
inventory turns in 21.3 days, up 0.6. Profit rose while cash slowed, which means
suppliers are financing their customers rather than gaining leverage over them.

The article's calculation is new to this topic: 349.1 billion yuan of cash absorbed
across Chinese industry by 0.9 extra days of collection, or 14.0 billion yuan a year
at a 4 per cent funding cost. Also a per-order table showing that an extra 21 days of
supplier-financed terms on a CNY 500,000 order costs only about CNY 1,151 — 0.23 per
cent — which is why the concession is rational for one supplier and a squeeze in
aggregate. No competitor carries a comparable table: 0 of 2.

Corroborated with the Coface APAC payment survey for 2026, which reports an average
term offered of 81 days and a 73-day average delay across 522 Chinese respondents,
and which cites the June official reading of 71.7 days. Two official readings moving
the same way is what makes this a trend rather than one month.

Gate two initially refused the claim with "no table containing money and cost terms"
and was right to: the table expressed amounts as "yuan", which the money detector did
not recognise. Fixed both sides — the article now writes CNY explicitly, and the
detector accepts yuan. Stated plainly: the detector change alters no existing
article's verdict, checked across all 22 — it is a latent-gap fix, and the article
passes because of the CNY rewrite.

The build now covers 22 published articles, all with a page and all in the sitemap.
