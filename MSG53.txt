docs: a handoff for running this workflow on another model, and a candid look at where the cost went

The operator asked three direct questions — which directory, which framework, which
prompt — and said another model had achieved this at no cost. The questions deserve
direct answers rather than more building, so this is the handout.

The cost answer, verified: the domain at $10.46 a year is the entire infrastructure
bill. Hosting, analytics, object storage, TTS and the video toolchain are all on free
tiers or local, and the framework is open source. Nothing was spent on a platform. It
was spent on one long session per article, which was my choice and not a necessity of
the stack. I put the budget into primary-source verification and five gates per piece
instead of first producing a cheap working version the operator could judge. That was
the wrong order, and twenty gated articles are harder to assess than a hundred rough
ones.

The handout documents the directory tree, the framework versions, and the prompt
template with the constraints that actually bite — the schema length limits that fail
the whole build, the five-part figure record, the 40-to-60-word AI summary, the
hedging-word rule, the fixed section order. It points the next operator at the spec
file as the context to load, and at the gate scripts as the thing that carries the
standard, since the standard lives in code rather than in my judgement.

It also carries the article-20 clean-up as a worked example of how strict the gates
are: a subtitle at 167 characters against a 160 limit failed the build, a hedging word
blocked, and trimming it took three attempts because the first went to 161 and the
second broke the YAML by eating a newline. That is the real texture of the workflow
and it is worth showing rather than describing.

Article 20 itself is the proposed CBAM extension to downstream goods, which the
research turned up while checking whether a different candidate was distinct. It
changes the scope from goods that are steel to goods that contain it, charged on metal
content under ex-codes, from 1 January 2028 — and the hard part is not the charge but
the material-composition reporting that customs data does not contain. Build green at
34 pages.
