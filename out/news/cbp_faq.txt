General Questions
When does the indefinite suspension of the de minimis exemption take effect for non-postal and postal shipments?
The suspension is effective on the date of publication in the Federal Register for both non-postal and postal shipments. The new mail informal entry process takes effect July 24, 2026.
Will the suspension of the de minimis exemption apply to products of all countries and all types of merchandise?
Yes, the suspension applies to merchandise valued at $800 or less arriving via all modes, including the international postal network, unless specifically excepted pursuant to a separate authority.
Are there any exceptions for bona fide gifts or personal articles accompanying travelers?
Yes. Exemptions for bona fide gifts (19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(A)) and personal or household articles accompanying travelers (19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(B)) remain unchanged.
Bona fide gifts as defined in 19 C.F.R § 10.153(a) are articles formerly owned by a donor who gave it outright in its entirety to a done without compensation or promise of compensation.
It does not include articles acquired by purchase, barter, promissory exchange, or similar transaction, nor does it include articles said to be “given” in conjunction with a purchase, barter, promissory exchange, or similar transaction, such as a so-called bonus article.
What entry types must be used for shipments valued at $800 or less that previously qualified for de minimis treatment?
Formal or informal entry procedures must be used. For mail shipments, the new mail informal entry process applies unless formal entry is required or elected.
What are the requirements for informal and formal entry procedures for low-value shipments under the new regulations?
Informal entry is generally allowed for shipments valued at $2,500 or less, subject to eligibility. Formal entry is required for shipments exceeding $2,500 and for certain types of merchandise (e.g., subject to quotas, AD/CVD).&nbsp;
Is military mail subject to duty?
No. Shipments sent to the U.S. from U.S. military addresses abroad, including Army Post Office (APO), Fleet Post Office (FPO) or Diplomatic Post Office (DPO) addresses, originate and remain in the domestic mail stream of the U.S. Postal Service.&nbsp;
Mail Informal Entry
What are the requirements for mail shipments to qualify for the new manual mail informal entry process?
Merchandise imported by mail that is valued at $2,500 or less, and is eligible for informal entry, may be entered under the new informal entry process.
However, effective October 22, 2026, the following merchandise is excluded from eligibility for the new mail informal entry process: merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed under Chapter 98 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS); merchandise subject to duties under Chapter 98 or Chapter 99 of the HTSUS; merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed pursuant to a Free Trade Agreement; and, merchandise subject to Partner Government Agency (PGA) requirements.
Merchandise ineligible for this new informal entry process must be entered under another appropriate entry process, such as an Entry Type 13 or formal entry.
Who is eligible to file entries under the new mail informal entry process?
Making entry under the new informal entry process is limited to parties with the right to make entry under 19 C.F.R. § 143.26(a): entry can only be made by an owner or purchaser of the merchandise being mailed to the United States, or a licensed customs broker properly designated by the owner, purchaser, or consignee.
What data elements are required to be filed for mail informal entries, and what is the proper method for submitting them?
Required data elements include:
*Filer Code
Value
Bond Number
Total Duty Owed
*Description of Merchandise
*Carrier
Country of Origin
Flight/Conveyance Number
*10-digit HTSUS classification(s)
Tracking Number (UPU S-10 bar code)
*Quantity/weight (if applicable)
Arrival Port
Duty Rate
Arrival Date
*Indicates new data elements&nbsp;
Submit via Excel or CSV spreadsheet to CBP by email no later than the 7th day of the month following arrival.
CSMS # 69183472 - Updated Global Guidance for International Mail
.
What is the S-10 bar code?
Under Universal Postal Union (UPU), Article 08‑002, the S-10 bar code is the standardized 13‑character alphanumeric identifier assigned to each postal item for international tracking and tracing. It is part of the UPU’s Identification of postal items.
Identification of postal items – 13-character identifier
.
Are there any additional data requirements?
For entries made before October 22, 2026, entry filers
may use the new mail informal entry process (in lieu of a formal entry)
and are required to submit any additional data mandated by an applicable regulation, statute, Executive Order, or Presidential Proclamation, for the following categories of merchandise: merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed under Chapter 98 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS); merchandise subject to duties under Chapter 98 or Chapter 99 of the HTSUS; merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed pursuant to a Free Trade Agreement; and, merchandise subject to Partner Government Agency (PGA) requirements.
Shipments exceeding $2,500 in value, subject to quota, or subject to antidumping or countervailing duties, are required to use formal entry procedures.
What are the bonding requirements for parties filing mail informal entries?
Making entry under the new mail informal entry process requires a basic importation and entry bond (single transaction or continuous), containing the conditions set forth in 19 C.F.R. § 113.62 (activity code 1). The bond must be effective and on file in ACE eBond prior to any activity occurring under the bond, and the bond must be sufficient to secure the transaction and ensure compliance with law and regulation.
How should gifts be declared?
Filers must clearly identify all gifts using the International Mail Duty (IMD) worksheet. When declaring a gift, follow the example on line 8 of the IMD worksheet. &nbsp;The worksheet can be found at
CSMS # 69183472 - Updated Global Guidance for International Mail
.
What should filers keep in mind when completing the interim spreadsheets?
File Format:
Submit your files as either .XLSX (Excel) or .CSV. Do not use the older .XLS format.
CSV Submissions:
If you are submitting a .CSV file, please include the data from the Pay.gov Worksheet (first tab) in the body of your email message.
Excel (.XLSX) Submissions:
Format your data as a table before submitting your Excel document.
To format your data as a table in Excel:
Select any cell within your data range.
Press Ctrl + T (or go to the "Insert" tab and click "Table").
Ensure the "Create Table" dialog box includes your full data range and that "My table has headers" is checked if your data has headers.
Click OK.
IMD Worksheet Tab:
Do not include blank rows (rows with no tracking number) in the IMD Worksheet tab.
Entry Type 13 Test
What is the Entry Type 13 Test?
The Entry Type 13 Test is a voluntary test conducted by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to evaluate a new electronic informal entry process for merchandise entering the United States via mail. It allows eligible parties to file informal mail entries electronically in the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE).
Why is CBP implementing this test?
CBP is implementing this test to automate and streamline the collection of informal entry data for mail shipments, especially after the indefinite suspension of the duty-free de minimis administrative exemption for mail shipments valued at $800 or less.
Who is eligible to participate in the Entry Type 13 Test?
Making entry under the new informal Entry Type 13 Test is limited to parties with the right to make entry under 19 C.F.R. § 143.26(a): entry can only be made by an owner or purchaser of the merchandise being mailed to the United States, or a licensed customs broker properly designated by the owner, purchaser, or consignee. &nbsp;In addition, carriers can also voluntarily participate by reporting the tracking number generated by a foreign postal operator for each arriving international mail shipment on a manifest.
What shipments are eligible for Entry Type 13?
All shipments valued at $2,500 or less sent to the United States via the international postal network are eligible for the Entry Type 13 Test. This includes merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed under Chapter 98 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS); merchandise subject to duties under Chapter 98 or Chapter 99 of the HTSUS; merchandise for which duty-free treatment is claimed pursuant to a Free Trade Agreement; and, merchandise subject to Partner Government Agency (PGA) requirements.
Shipments exceeding $2,500 in value, subject to quota, or subject to antidumping or countervailing duties, are required to use formal entry procedures.
What data elements are required to be filed for the Entry Type 13 Test?
Required data elements include:
Filer code
Value
IOR number
Total duty owed
Description of merchandise
Carrier name
Country of Origin
Tracking number (UPU S-10 bar code)
All applicable 10-digit HTSUS classification(s)
Arrival port
Quantity and weight (if using specific duty rates)
Duty Rate
Additional data may be required for shipments subject to PGA requirements or special duties.
Is a bond required for the Entry Type 13 Test?
Making entry under the Entry Type 13 process requires a basic importation and entry bond (single transaction or continuous), containing the conditions set forth in 19 C.F.R. § 113.62 (activity code 1). The bond must be effective and on file in ACE eBond prior to any activity occurring under the bond, and the bond must be sufficient to secure the transaction and ensure compliance with law and regulation.
What happens if a participant fails to comply with test requirements?
Participants who fail to follow the test rules or applicable laws may be subject to civil and criminal penalties, administrative sanctions, liquidated damages, or other enforcement actions.
How long will the test run?
The test will commence 90 days after publication in the Federal Register and will continue until CBP announces its conclusion in the Federal Register.
How can I submit comments or feedback?
Comments can be submitted via email to
cbpdm@cbp.dhs.gov
at any time during the test. Use "Comments on the Entry Type 13 Test" in the subject line.
Duty, Fees and Revenue
What duties, taxes, and fees will apply to low-value shipments under the new rules?
All applicable duties, taxes, fees, exactions, and charges must be paid on these shipments, based on HTSUS classification, country of origin, dutiable value, and other relevant criteria.
How will duty rates be determined for mail shipments under the new process?
Duty rates are based on the HTSUS classification and the rates in effect at the time the entry is made.
Will the filer be required to pay duties on the mail informal entry at the time of entry, or can payment be deferred?
Payment must be transmitted via
Pay.gov
no later than the 7th day of the month following arrival.
How will CBP handle shipments that arrive without the required data or bond?
Such shipments will not be released from CBP custody until all requirements are satisfied.
Will there be any changes to the Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF) or other fees for low-value shipments?
Other than&nbsp;items that are sent to the United States through the international postal network by “Inbound Express Mail service” or “Inbound EMS” (as that service is described in the mail classification schedule referred to in section 3631 of title 39), mail shipments are exempt from MPF. Other fees may apply as determined by CBP and the applicable regulations.
Operational and Practical Questions
What is the process for submitting the required International Mail Duty Worksheet for mail informal entries, and what is the deadline for submission?
The worksheet must be emailed to CBP (
CBPDM@cbp.dhs.gov
) no later than the 7th day of the month following the package’s arrival.
What happens if a shipment arrives and the filer does not have a bond or does not have the right to make entry?
The shipment will not be released from CBP custody until a filer submits the entry and the required bond is transmitted.
How will CBP handle shipments in transition during the effective dates of the new rules?
Until the new mail informal entry process takes effect, importers may use the interim process provided for in Executive Order 14324, as amended by E.O. 14388.
